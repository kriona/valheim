# Viking Villages

NOTE: this mod has not been released, there are no existing villages, and breaking changes just mean I need to delete my test worlds.

The Meadows' abandoned farms aren't abandoned anymore. Living Vikings move in, keep their village in repair, go about their lives, fight off whatever comes for them and come back after they die.

Viking Villages works on its own, and with [Zombie Apocalypse](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Kriona/ZombieApocalypse/) the two know about each other - see With Zombie Apocalypse below.

## Living Vikings

Living Vikings hold out in the Meadows' farms.

- Each farmhouse of a farm is home to 2 living Vikings, never starred, who move in once its bonfire is built, the first time the whole village has loaded around you - all at once, in the village beside its houses, whether you can see them or not - and the Vikings keep it in repair - none of its walls, floors or roofs start out damaged, and none of its roof tiles, wall tops or doors are missing. Nothing of it falls down for want of support on uneven ground, either: a fence section left floating is lowered onto the ground, or taken away when the ground falls more than 2 meters under it, and a house's floor, wall, stair or pole that reaches neither the ground nor a piece under it gets a column of stone blocks under it, down into the ground - put right once its paths are laid, with nothing of the farm wearing until then. In the Deep North, the deep snow on its ground is cleared as its paths are laid - within 8 meters of its fence and its houses, outposts included - and the snow its pieces start out buried under is knocked off; after that, the Vikings clear off the snow that builds up on its pieces and any deep snow left on its ground (see Clear snow below). A farmhouse that would have had a wild beehive hanging inside, stinging the Vikings, has a beehive for honey standing outside instead
- A living Viking is a Viking of their village's biome - their health and skills rolled from their biome's range - from 50 health and skills of 0-12 in the Meadows to 1200 health and skills of 88-100 in the Deep North, each skill rolled on its own - and their gear all their biome's own (a piece their biome has none of, like a bow, still comes from the nearest biome below that has one), and from the Mountains on they always wear their set's helmet - and a village counts as the hardest biome within 50 meters of its farm's middle - from the Meadows through the Black Forest, the Swamp, the Mountains, the Plains, the Mistlands and the Ashlands to the Deep North - so a Meadows village by the Black Forest has the Black Forest's Vikings
- A living Viking starts with their own level in each skill, rolled within their biome's range - every Viking is at least level 1 in every skill - and their skills rise the way yours do - each hit with a weapon raises that weapon's skill, each block their Blocking, each second spent running or swimming their Run or Swim, each jump their Jump, each piece of food put on or taken off a cooking station their Cooking, and each batch of arrows crafted or piece of gear repaired their Crafting, at the same rate as yours, sped up by the world's skill gain setting and by being rested. Run and Jump work like yours too - up to 25% faster running and 40% stronger jumps at full skill - and their gear speeds them up or slows them down the way yours does, from a Fenris piece's bonus to iron armor's, a heavy weapon's or a tower shield's penalty, counting half again as much when they run, and nothing for a weapon they've put away - and Cooking and Crafting give the bonus yours do, a chance of one more cooked piece or arrow, up to a quarter of the time at full skill. Swim, which only eases your stamina, makes them swim up to 25% faster at full skill instead, since they don't tire. The higher a skill, the harder they hit and the better they block, the same as you. When one of their skills reaches a new level, they glow and chime around their head like you do, which every player nearby sees and hears - the chime at half your own level-up's volume, coming from them and fading with distance, so you don't take it for yours - and say how proud they are, at most every 30 seconds, in a line nothing else they say covers up while it shows - always within 10 meters of a player, and less often the farther they are from the nearest one, down to never at 40 meters, where nobody would see it. A Viking who comes back after dying starts over, while one who rises as a zombie with Zombie Apocalypse keeps their skills
- A village's core is its farmhouses and the empty houses close by them - within 15 meters, wall to wall, of a house already in the core. Its Vikings live only there. The empty houses standing off on their own, or only near each other, are outposts - fixed up and furnished, but no one's home, and the Vikings only go out to them by day, to repair them, put loot away or use their chests. A farm's village reaches 2 meters past its fence, taking in its pens and fields along with its houses (or 8 meters past the walls of its core houses when one stands farther out, or on a farm generated before the mod noted its fence's size), and, separately, 8 meters past the walls of each outpost or group of outposts close together, so the open ground between the core and an outpost off on its own isn't the village's - an enemy out there isn't in the village, and loot lying there isn't the village's to pick up. A village you started counts its bonfire and the houses close by it as its core
- Each farm is generated at its own size, picked at random, so villages come in a range of sizes - its fence anywhere from 24 to 44 meters from its middle, where the game's is 18-32, and from 36 to 44 meters for a farm in or within 150 meters of any biome harder than the Meadows, like the Black Forest - so its houses, fields and pens spread out in a large one, and the trees, bushes, rocks and logs within 6 meters past its fence are cleared away - branches, stones, berries and the like are left. Its bonfire stands on open ground in its very middle, with no building within 7 meters of it, its farmhouses around it, within 45% of the fence's distance from the middle or 15 meters, whichever is farther, and its pens out toward the fence, past 65% of that distance - its fields and the rest go anywhere else. It gets as many more fields, pens and the rest as it takes in more ground, so it's as full as the game's own, but no more farmhouses
- Half of a farm's farmhouses, picked at random, are built from the house designs in the mod's `Blueprints` folder instead, when it has any - PlanBuild `.blueprint` files, which PlanBuild and Infinity Hammer both save. Each stands in the farmhouses' ring on ground that rises and falls no more than a meter under it, its doors turned toward the bonfire, and keeps the furniture it was designed with rather than being furnished. A design with no door isn't used. The `kri_vv_blueprints` console command lists the designs and anything wrong with them, and `kri_vv_blueprints reload` reads them again, for farms generated after
- Every village has at least 3 houses in its core, or 4 for a farm in or within 150 meters of any biome harder than the Meadows, like the Black Forest - a farm generated with fewer, counting its farmhouses and the empty houses close by them, gets more farmhouses inside its fence, or just outside it when there's no room inside
- The houses are furnished the first time the whole village has loaded around you - in each, a wood bed for every Viking who calls it home (so none in an outpost), a wood chest or two - the village's chests start out with a full stack each of wood, stone and resin, 10 each of two of its biome's foods - like cooked boar meat and raspberries in the Meadows - so the Vikings have something to eat before they forage, hunt and cook their own, and 2 pickaxes of its biome - antler in the Meadows and the Black Forest, bronze in the Swamp, iron in the Mountains and the Plains, and black metal from the Mistlands on - each in a chest picked at random - and a wood table with 2-4 stools around it, and the village gets a workbench and a forge in its core houses - each in a different house when there are enough - unless one already stands somewhere in the village, whoever built it. Every village has both: one missing either - with no room for it when it was furnished, or a core of houses built from blueprints - gets it right then, or the first time an older village loads, and only then, so none appears later on: in a house of its core with room for it, or else in one built from a blueprint, or else out on flat open ground in the village, away from its fire, under a little shelter of its own - a wall behind it and at each end and a wood roof, open at the front - which covers it enough to use. With no flat ground for it, the ground is flattened for it, clearing the trees, logs, bushes and rocks in the way, and when even that leaves nowhere, the shelter stands on a wooden platform at the flattest spot - floors with room to stand in front, posts down to the ground and stairs down from the front. Houses and spots out of your sight are tried first. The village's own workbench and forge, and any black forge or galdr table its houses came with, are then upgraded far enough to repair the gear of its biome and every biome before it: a level 2 workbench in the Meadows and level 3 from the Black Forest on, a forge of level 2 in the Swamp, 3 in the Mountains and 4 from the Plains on, a level 4 black forge from the Ashlands on, and a galdr table of level 2 in the Ashlands and 4 in the Deep North. The upgrades a station is missing go within its reach, counting any it already has: against a wall of its house, as close as fits, or on level ground beside its shelter, keeping the floor in front of every station clear. An older village gets them the first time it loads, and only then. The Vikings, like you, can only use a workbench or forge with a roof over it. Beds, chests, the workbench and the forge stand against the walls, the table in the open middle. Only what fits goes in - the way from the door to every piece stays clear and at least half the floor stays free, so a small house gets fewer pieces, and a Viking without a bed sits on a stool or the floor at night. The Vikings repair and rebuild the furniture like the rest of their village. A bed the houses came with that's only for show, which you can't use or claim, is swapped for a real one where it stands the first time the village loads - the game's bed of the same kind when there is one, like a dragon bed for a prop dragon bed, or else a wood bed
- An abandoned house with no creatures of its own within 100 meters of a farm is taken in by its Vikings, and fixed up the same way, with its vines cleared away. One in the village's core is home to 2 more Vikings, who move in along with the farmhouses' Vikings. A village generated when its houses held 1-2 Vikings each is short the Vikings it now holds, and gets them back over the days the way it gets back its dead
- The ground around the farm and its houses is flattened the first time the whole village has loaded around you - its bumps smoothed out and its slopes eased, leaving the ground right around buildings as it is, with trees, rocks and bushes moved up or down with the ground. Dry ground is never lowered into the water - it stops half a meter above it - so a village on low ground, like the Plains by the sea, doesn't flood. A village in the Swamp or the Plains has its ground raised out of any water it stands in, up to half a meter above it - fully within 30 meters of its middle and 10 of its houses, sloping back down into the swamp by 44 and 20 meters - by at most 3 meters, leaving the ground right around buildings as it is, and its paths cross the raised ground rather than bridge it
- Dirt paths join the farmhouses and the houses near the farm, right up to their doors, laid the first time the whole village has loaded around you. They wind around buildings, fences, large trees, bushes and large rocks, keep to flat ground, and clear away the branches, stones, small trees and small rocks in their way. A path crosses a river or stream where it's narrow, on a bridge resting on both banks, and goes around water too wide to bridge - villages whose paths were laid before bridges get theirs the next time they load. A bridge is of wood floors, up to 14 meters across, or in the Ashlands and the Deep North of grausten floors, which hold each other up over a shorter span than wood, so a grausten bridge reaches only as far as they do. In the Ashlands, the lava and the boiling sea count as water - the paths go around them, crossing only on a bridge, and the village's bonfire and portal never stand on them. Where there's no way around, a path goes through as little of what's in its way as it can, so every house is joined. The ground under the houses' floors is painted the same way, so grass doesn't grow through them
- Two villages whose farms are within 100 meters of each other are joined by a path between their nearest farmhouses
- A bonfire stands on the open ground nearest the middle of the farm, clear of its buildings and paths, built along with the paths and stacked with as much wood as it holds. Sloping ground is flattened for it. While it burns it pushes back the fog - the nearer you are to it, the thinner the weather's fog, clearing it fully within 15 meters and back to all of it 45 meters out, thinning and thickening over a few seconds as the fire lights or goes out - except in the Mistlands, whose mist a fire doesn't touch. The village's fire crackles and roars at half the game's loudness, since it burns all night beside where the Vikings sleep - a bonfire or campfire you build that they take up as theirs too, and it's back to full once it's no longer theirs. A farm with no room for a bonfire within 30 meters of its middle stays an abandoned farm - no fire, paths, bridges, flattened ground or furniture, and no living Vikings ever move in
- Once the fire is lit, the village gets cooking stations over it - two back to back across a bonfire, or one over a campfire - each as far out from the fire's middle as it can stand and still cook. A village from the Swamp on gets iron cooking stations, which also cook what the Swamp's and later biomes' hunts bring in. A village's lit bonfire runs any cooking station sitting on it - yours too - as long as part of the station is in its flames, even one that would otherwise need a hearth or several campfires under it. A village with a campfire gets a wooden station when an iron one won't fit over it. A village from the Black Forest on also gets a cauldron in the flames beside them, with more of the cauldron's extensions around it for each biome after the Black Forest - the spice rack for the Swamp, then the butcher's table, the pots and pans, the mortar and pestle, the rolling pins, and the smoker for the Deep North. Two sitting logs go around the fire too, facing it, where there's room for them - on dry, fairly flat ground with nothing in the way, outside the houses and off the paths, within the fire's warmth and spread around it. The Vikings rebuild them like the rest of their village
- The farm's blank signposts are signs with a name on each, picked at random from these and from the names the world's other villages go by, wherever they are - though only one of a village's signs points to another village. A sign with a wooden post through it is moved off the post, facing away from it, so its text isn't cut off. You can write your own over it, like any sign, and a sign the Vikings build back gets a new name
  - Realms: Valhalla, Asgard, Midgard, Helheim, Jotunheim, Niflheim, Bifrost, Yggdrasil
  - Bosses: Eikthyr, Elder
  - Biomes: Black Forest, Swamp, Plains, Mistlands, Deep North
  - Places: Mead Hall, Burial Chambers, Troll Cave
- A village in the Ashlands or the Deep North comes with a portal home, put up with its paths: a stone portal on open ground out past the houses nearest its middle, at least 16 meters from it, joined to a wood portal out the same way in a village in the Plains or an easier biome. The other village is one of the world's farms no one has been near yet, picked at random - or, with none left, a new farm in a part of the world no one has been to, or, with the whole world explored, a new farm somewhere with nothing anyone built and no other location within 150 meters. Its farm is generated and its village named right away, so you can go through before anyone has been there - its portal waits in the middle of the farm until the village loads around someone, then moves out to its own open ground. Both portals are named after the Ashlands or Deep North village - cut short to 10 characters, as many as you can type for a portal, with #2, #3 and so on after the name when another portal has it already - each has a dirt path to the nearest of its village's paths and a sign on its frame naming the village on the other side, and neither stands on lava. When there's nowhere left for another village, the Ashlands or Deep North village has no portal. Each portal and its sign are the village's own, like the pieces it was generated with: they break into what they're built of, and the Vikings build them back, linked to the same village, with that village's name on the sign as it goes by now - though only while that village is still there and wasn't abandoned for want of room for its bonfire, and the sign only once its portal stands. The Vikings of both villages talk about the village through their portal and its biome, among themselves and now and then to you
- A village in the Mistlands is lit against the mist, put up with its paths: a wisp fountain, drawing wisps you can pick, on open ground at least 8 meters from its fire, and wisp torches - standing torches that push back the mist - 4 around the fire 8 meters out, one every 14 meters around the inside of its fence, and more along its paths wherever a path runs more than 10 meters from any light, each on open ground beside the path. They're the village's own, like the pieces it was generated with: they break into what they're built of, and the Vikings build back those within the village
- The boars in a farm's pens are the Vikings' livestock, tamed as they spawn while a living Viking is within 40 meters, or within a second afterwards while the village has living Vikings - they leave the Vikings and you alone, and fight off zombies and monsters that come within 12 meters of where they spawned, standing their ground rather than running from them as a wild boar would, even by a fire. One a Viking slaughters, or hits by accident, neither runs nor fights back. The Vikings only go for one when they're slaughtering it - even a Viking angry at you leaves the rest alone. Wild boars that wander into the village stay wild. They're the Vikings' to keep, so you can't pet, rename or breed them. Once the farm's Vikings are gone, its boars spawn wild. A pen whose boar is killed gets a new one once its spawner's wait is up - 20 minutes for one the game gives no wait, which would never spawn again - while you're nearby, even inside the village, where a fire or a bed would keep the game's spawners from spawning
- They're armed for their biome and always wear armor (the set's chest and legs) and wield a weapon - the set's helmet and cape (50% each) and a shield (40%, unless their weapon takes both hands) are left to chance, but for the helmet and cape from the Mountains on, and everything they have is fully repaired. From the Mountains on, every Viking wears a cape - the set's, or else the biome's own (a wolf fur cape in the Mountains, a lox cape in the Plains, a feather cape in the Mistlands, an Asksvin cloak in the Ashlands and a moose hide cape in the Deep North). A Viking with a bow or crossbow carries 100 arrows or bolts of their biome, and never carries weapons or ammo that deal spirit damage, or Dyrnwyn in the Deep North. Their armor protects them as well as yours does you and wears down the same way, their weapons and shield wear down as they use them, and they raise their shield against attacks, more often and with better timing the more skilled they are. They know the reach of what they fight with, closing in until their swing will land before they strike - right up close with a knife or their fists, farther off with a polearm - and swing as fast as you do, again as soon as their last swing ends, stringing their weapon's combo together. Every cape of the Mountains and the Deep North resists frost, so their Vikings don't freeze there. They carry no coins, so a living Viking who dies for good drops none
- Spawn one with the console command `spawn Viking` - near a village they join it, and out in the wild they're lost for a little while before setting out for the nearest village (see Vikings With No Village below)
- With `debugmode` on, a living Viking's job shows in brackets after their name, in light gray - over their health bar, when you look at them, and on markers from mods that show their name - and looking at any Viking shows what they're doing - their health, target, whether they're alerted, aggravated, asleep or in danger, what they hold, and for a living Viking their ID - a GUID of their own, apart from their name, which the news they've told goes by - their village, home, job and how long they've been at it, how long they've been standing still when it's more than 10 seconds, whether they're ready for a raid, the foods they've eaten with how long each has left and how much they heal them, whether they're resting by a fire or rested and for how long, how much faster that makes them heal, the forsaken powers they know, which they're under and when they can use one again, what's moving them and whether they're twitching - stopping and starting or turning back and forth - which is also written to the log with what they were doing, and the loot lying around the village - how much it would take, and why it leaves the rest. Looking at a village's bonfire shows the village's state - which client runs it, its houses, the Vikings loaded and how many are hurt or hostile, its head count and the Vikings it's waiting to get back and when the next is due, tonight's watch, the fire's fuel and cooking stations, its pieces noted, missing and damaged, what's still to be set up, whether enemies are near, the beds players built for it - how many brought a Viking, and why the rest haven't yet - and the raid going on, if any: where it is, how far past the village's edge, whether it's running or paused with no player near, and whether it's on the village - and a path a house added for a player's bed still needs, with who's making it or what it's waiting on. What would only say there's nothing to report is left out, like tonight's watch by day before one is picked, counts of none, and enemies when there are none near. Hover text longer than 12 lines is shown twice as wide and centered on the crosshair, so it stays on screen at the same size
- A monster lying asleep until someone comes near - like an abomination disguised as roots and logs in the Swamp - wakes for a living Viking coming as close as you would wake it, and goes for them, so the Vikings fight it rather than walk past it
- Like the dvergr, they fight zombies and monsters and leave you alone - until you hurt one, which turns every Viking and dvergr nearby against you. They don't pick fights they don't need, though: a living Viking only goes after a zombie or monster that's a threat - one in their village, within 10 meters of them, alerted, or after someone - and leaves the rest alone unless they attack the Viking or someone the Viking is called to help. A leech is never a threat until it hurts you or a Viking, however near it lies, so they don't wade into the Swamp's water after one. Unlike the dvergr, a Viking calms down again once they've slept on it - one angered by day forgives you the next morning, and one angered at night as the next night falls, as long as no player has hurt them since
- You can only hurt a living Viking with PvP on (the PvP toggle on your inventory screen), the same as hurting another player, so a stray swing or arrow with PvP off won't turn the village against you. The first time a hit of yours does nothing because PvP is off, Hugin flies in to explain, once per character. A Viking that's already hostile can always be fought back. The console's kill commands, and the K key in `debugmode`, only kill the living Vikings who are angry at you while your PvP is off, and every living Viking with it on - they say how many they spared, and the first time they spare any, Hugin flies in to explain. This covers kill commands other mods put in place of the game's too, like Server Devcommands'
- They don't groan like the draugr
- They walk while all is quiet at home, and run when there's danger - when they've spotted an enemy or been hurt in the last 10 seconds - and whenever they're out of the village's core, so they're out in the open for as little time as they can be. In the rain they hurry everywhere out from under a roof, back to a walk once they're under one. They also run up any slope of 20 degrees or more, so they make it up
- While all is quiet they carry their weapon and shield sheathed on their back, and draw them as soon as there's danger
- When it's dark they pull out a torch while they aren't fighting, beside a one-handed weapon or in place of another - from just before sunset until dawn, while they're away from their village's fire and its gathering fires and not in bed - and put it away by day. In a fight they put it away for a shield or a two-handed weapon, but keep it beside a one-handed weapon with nothing else in their off hand - and one that starts a fight without a torch out, like a Viking woken by the night watch, pulls one out there
- Each living Viking starts with a torch of their own, which burns down while they hold it, the same as yours - about a night's worth - and is gone once it burns out. Once their only torch is half burnt, or they have none, they craft another from its recipe, with wood and resin from the village's chests, using up the more burnt one first. When the village has no resin, they go hunting greylings for it, one Viking at a time. At dusk and at night they craft one too, from what they carry and the chests hold rather than going out for it in the dark: one with no torch at all before going to bed, and the night watch, before keeping watch, whenever their torches don't have enough burn left between them to last the rest of their shift - including partway through it, when they stop patrolling to craft one. A Viking with no torch and nothing to make one with goes without, trying again a minute later
- Some Vikings start with a Dverger circlet in place of a torch, more of them the harder their biome: none in the Meadows, 10% in the Black Forest, 30% in the Swamp, 50% in the Mountains, 70% in the Plains and every one from the Mistlands on. Whenever they'd pull out a torch, they wear the circlet in place of their helmet instead, and put their helmet back on by day, in bed and as soon as there's danger. A Viking with a circlet never crafts a torch, and one without takes up a circlet they find as loot or in the village's chests
- A Viking of the Mistlands starts with a wisp light worn on their belt, which floats beside them and pushes back the mist the way yours does
- In the Mistlands' mist, a Viking sees only 10 meters, as the Dvergr do - unless they or what they're looking at are within reach of a wisp light, a wisp torch or one of the Dvergr's demisters - though they still hear you coming as far off as always
- Whatever a Viking crafts - arrows, a torch, a hoe - when the village is short of something it takes, they go and get it: foraging for it when it's picked, like flint or stone, or else hunting only the prey or birds that drop it, like greylings for resin, boars for leather or birds for feathers, one Viking at a time for each. With nothing wild about that drops it, they slaughter one of the village's livestock that does, which its pen brings back. An archer with arrows left gets what the best arrows their station allows take before crafting worse ones, and one with none crafts what they can straight away

### Village Life

The living Vikings go about their lives in their village, and drop everything to fight when danger comes.

- While you're in a village, your game runs everything in it and up to 80 meters past its edge - its Vikings, the monsters they fight, its animals, fires and dropped items - wherever it is, as long as it's loaded around you. The game itself only runs what's within 64 to 128 meters of you, depending on where you stand, so without this a Viking working at an outpost past that would stand frozen there with the monster they were fighting until you came closer. In multiplayer, each player runs the village they're in, and a player already running something keeps it.

- Vikings see 50 meters. Out of the village - foraging, hunting, or getting wood or loot - a Viking leaves alone whatever has a monster within 50 meters of it, and heads home as soon as one comes that close or they end up with an enemy to fight, so they're back before most monsters spot them rather than leading a pack home. A single greyling with no other monster within 50 meters doesn't count - a Viking carries on with what they're doing and fights it where they are.
- When a Viking or player within 30 meters is attacked by a zombie or monster, the Vikings rush to help, whatever they're doing - even asleep. They don't chase an enemy more than 40 meters past the village's edge (70 meters for one that hurt them or someone they're defending), or into the water - they stop at the shore, and leave alone an enemy out in water more than a meter deep, like a Neck swimming in a river, though archers still shoot at it from the shore - and walk back when they end up far outside it
- They don't fight a losing fight away from home. Outside the village's core, a Viking runs back to its middle for help when they're attacked with no other Viking within 15 meters, when 4 or more enemies are on them, when they're below a quarter of their health, or when a Viking with them is - which brings their companions home with them. One who ran for being alone turns to fight as soon as another Viking is with them, and the rest once they're home with another Viking of their village within 6 meters - home with no one beside them, a Viking runs on to the nearest of their fellows rather than turning to fight alone at the edge of the village, and only stands alone when none of them is left
- At home, a Viking below a quarter of their health hangs back and lets the others take the fight, until they're back above 40% - unless there are more enemies than healthy Vikings near, or none of them are healthy, when they rejoin the fight. Hanging back, they don't just watch: they circle around behind an enemy in the village's core that's busy with someone else, never crossing in front of it, and strike it from behind. As soon as an enemy comes after them, they run - keeping about 5 meters from the fight, or 20 meters from an enemy that shoots or throws, like a greydwarf, a greydwarf shaman or a zombie Viking with a bow - and go back to striking from behind 3 seconds after nothing's after them
- A Viking who goes after an enemy calls it out by name - "Watch out - Neck!" - at most every 15 seconds, and only when no other Viking has called out that enemy in the last minute
- When you come into a village after being away from its Vikings for 3 minutes, the first Viking within 10 meters who sees you turns, waves and greets you - just the one, so the whole village doesn't. One asleep or after an enemy doesn't. Each Viking remembers the players they've greeted: one who has never met you greets you as a stranger, and one who has greets you by name and welcomes you back
- Look at a living Viking and press the use key (E) to talk to them. One who has never met you greets you as a stranger and remembers you, one who knows you answers you by name, one asleep in bed mumbles, and one in a fight is too busy. Whatever they say, they stop and stand facing you for 4 seconds while you read it. Now and then one who knows you asks how you're doing, or talks about one of the runestones of the biome they're standing in - the boar stone of the Meadows, Ulf's stones and the rest. A Viking who shares a name with someone on the runestones, like Ulf, Astrid, Bjørn, Harald or Gudrun, sometimes talks about the stones of the one with their name
- Each Viking remembers when they met you - when they greeted you or you talked to them for the first time - and how many times you've talked to them, counting talks at least a minute apart. Once they've known you for 30 game days, or you've talked to them more than 10 times, you're old friends, and now and then they greet you or answer you as one
- Look at a living Viking and press I to open their inventory beside yours - yours on the left, under your character card, and theirs on the right, under a card of their own with their name and a Skills button that shows their skills, with no crafting panel. Their armor and weight show in tabs on the left of their inventory, the way yours show on the right of yours, and both of you get a health tab between them - your health and theirs, now and at most, marked with the heart a tamed animal shows as you pet it - and their status effects show under it the way yours show on your HUD - each one's icon, name and time left, including resting, rested, wet, cold and poisoned - with yours shown the same way under your inventory. The window goes back to its usual layout once it closes. What they wear - equipped, or sheathed on their back - is marked the way your equipped items are, and their weight shows with the most they can carry, 300. When they're due, they all walk back in together from where they left, however long apart they left the village - those who left first wait for the rest, giving up on one a few minutes after they're due when it never left, killed on its way out, say - and put what they brought home in the chests before anything else, even at night, calling their own houses home again. The beds that brought them are free again, and the bonfire stays as an ordinary fire. Start a village at that bonfire again and every Viking who hasn't settled in another village yet comes back, as they were when they left - their name, gear, inventory and skills, and the players they know - one to each unclaimed bed near it, as the beds call for Vikings, before any new Viking comes. Those still gathering or near enough to see stop and turn back - one near you says so - and walk home, each taking the first unclaimed bed that calls for a Viking, ahead of those coming back from farther off. Those already farther off go on to the village they were going to, say so when they get there, and turn around for home - a message says how many are on their way and roughly when, in game time, like "3 Vikings are on the way back - expect them in a couple of hours". One coming home to a village with no bed for them waits there 5 minutes, then comes to its beds like the rest. Those who left with no village to go to come back right away. Destroy the bonfire, and those not on the road are gone for good

### Vikings With No Village

A living Viking who belongs to no village and isn't near one - spawned out in the wild, or leaving a village you disbanded - makes for the village they can get to soonest, over land and sea and through portals.

- One you spawn out in the wild with a console command - `spawn Viking`, or another mod's spawn command - is lost at first: they stand still for 3 seconds, then turn to look a new way every 3-4 seconds, for 15-20 seconds in all. A second or two after they appear they say something like "Where am I?" or "Am I in Valhalla?", and half the time something else 7 seconds later. They can still greet you, the way a traveler does - lost, they ask you where they are. One spawned another way skips straight to finding a village
- They pick the village of their own biome or easier that they can get to soonest, anywhere in the world: one of the Meadows' farms, whether it has been generated yet or not - but for one that stayed abandoned with no room for a bonfire - or a village a player started. They go on foot, through rivers and swamp water, and cross the sea by boat - the best boat their biome has: a raft from the Meadows, a karve from the Black Forest and the Swamp, a longship from the Mountains to the Mistlands, and a drakkar from the Ashlands on - but never the Ashlands' lava or the edge of the world, and never the Ashlands' boiling sea in a boat it would burn, which only the drakkar holds up in. They also go through any portal joined to another, yours or a village's, when that gets them there sooner - but not while one of them carries something a portal won't take, like ore, unless the portal takes everything or the world lets every portal, and never in a world with portals turned off. So the village they pick is the soonest there by land, sea and portal, not the nearest in a straight line. The server plans the road over the world's map, ground no one has been near included, keeping out of biomes harder than theirs where there's a way around. A group that sets out together keeps out of biomes harder than the easiest of them and sails in the best boat of the hardest of them
- On the road they run from waypoint to waypoint, with a torch out at night, and stay out of every fight they can - running from an enemy that comes within 30 meters, along their road when that leads away from it - unless they can't get away: the enemy is faster than them, they're cornered, or it's hurting them up close. They stand and fight then, and join in when a player or another living Viking within 15 meters is attacked. A group that set out together waits for anyone who falls 15 meters behind. They eat the food they carry and pick food growing within 25 meters when they have none they can eat
- Now and then, with you near, they say things like "I need to get to Eikvik." - a farm not set up yet is named by the name settled for it - and they pass on your news when they greet you, but say none of a village's lines
- A Viking who reaches their village joins it once it has had every Viking it starts with, waiting by it while a farm no one has been near is set up. One who reaches a farm that stayed abandoned, or a village that's gone, says so and moves on to the next nearest - or, for one who left a village you disbanded, to a village picked at random - and one who waits 5 minutes by a village that should take them but has no house for them moves on to the next nearest
- For 12 game hours after they get where they were going - a village they join, or home from a quest - they talk about the trip, to you and to the others, along with how their quest went: the thing most worth telling, from being wrecked at sea, turning around for home, finding their village gone or a broken portal on their road, to a portal they stepped through, the boat they sailed in, the hardest biome they walked through or what they picked on the way
- With no village they can reach, they wander - strolling a short way at a time over ground no harder than their biome, away from starred monsters and those much tougher than them, fighting what they can take on, picking food and picking up loot lying within 10 meters outside any player's base - and look for a village again every 3 minutes
- Where their road reaches the sea, they wait at the shore for the rest of their group, for up to 30 seconds, then the one of the hardest biome among them takes out a hammer and builds the boats they need from nothing - the best boat of their biome, as many as it takes for all of them (a raft carries 6, a karve 10, a longship 25 and a drakkar 44), with one of them at each helm, one holding on to the mast - and on the longship another holding on at the figurehead - and the rest on the ship's stools, then sitting on the boards wherever there's room - on the longship's cargo, and spread evenly along the drakkar's steps - a little crowded on the smaller boats. The boats drop nothing when they break. Each Viking tries once to jump on board from the shore and swims to the ladder if they miss, and those not on board after 30 seconds are put there. Once they're all on, the boat waits for you if you're standing near - 5 seconds later the Viking at the helm asks if you're coming, and 15 seconds after that it sets off with or without you. You can ride along, but the helm is theirs. Walk into a Viking sitting on the boards and they scoot out of your way, still sitting, up to a little over a meter - never off the deck or into the mast, the rail or anything else on board - and slide slowly back to their seat once you've moved on. Those at the helm, holding on to the mast or the figurehead, or on a stool or the drakkar's steps stay put. The boat sails itself along their road with a fair wind of its own, whatever the weather, backing off when it gets stuck. At the far shore they all jump off and swim in, and the one who was at the helm breaks the boat up with their hammer - waiting for you to get off first, for up to a minute. If the boat is wrecked at sea, they swim for the nearest shore, as long as their stamina would last you - by their food and Swim skill - and drown the way you would after that, and once ashore they plan their road again, building another boat if there's still sea to cross. Log out on their boat in single player and it's all still there when you come back. Where there's no water deep enough for a boat by the shore, they say they're setting sail and are taken off the road to sail the rest of the way. Where it reaches a portal, they walk up to it, say they're stepping through, and once all of them are there step through together - coming out the other side right away when a player is near it, or else taken off the road the same way. A group is never split up: as soon as one of them goes out of every player's reach, the whole group is taken off the road. The server follows them along their road while they're off it, so when a portal on the way is broken or no longer joined to its other end, they find another way from wherever they've got to. The server keeps them until they'd have covered the rest of the way - running at about 5 meters a second, sailing at their boat's speed, from about 3 meters a second for a raft to 8.5 for a drakkar, and 5 seconds for each portal - a group as one, so they arrive together. Once they're due and their village is loaded and set up, they walk in from the side they came from, out of sight, as they were - with their gear, inventory, skills and the players they know, and a few things they picked on the way: one for every 3 minutes they walked through a biome where something grows - raspberries and mushrooms in the Meadows, blueberries, mushrooms, yellow mushrooms and thistle in the Black Forest, cloudberries in the Plains, Jotun puffs and magecaps in the Mistlands, fiddleheads and smoke puffs in the Ashlands - up to 5 each, less one they ate for every 15 minutes on the road. Vikings out to mine or raid only pick on the way home. A village no one goes near keeps them waiting. One who dies on the road is gone for good

### News

Word of what you do gets around, and the living Vikings pass it on - each piece of news told by 2-4 Vikings, from any village, each in a line of their own, in place of their greeting or what they'd say when you talk to them (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)).

- News is made when you kill a boss or a sea serpent (the Bonemaw too), drop a tree on a monster - a boss included - sail within 30 meters of a leviathan, die, or sail off the edge of the world, which the Vikings only admire you for and don't count as a death
- And by your character's firsts in the world: the first time they kill each of Hildir's minibosses (Brenna, Geirrhafa, and Zil and Thungr once both are down), talk to or trade with Haldor, Hildir and the Bog Witch, go into a burial chamber, troll cave or frost cave, discover each biome but the Meadows - when the game says you've discovered it, counting a biome's alternate versions as the biome itself - tame each kind of animal, take each skill to 100, have every skill at 100, and earn each of these achievements: The Lumberjack, The Cook, The Farmer, The Forager, The Beekeeper, No Adventuring On an Empty Stomach, The Hunter, The Trophy Collector, The Fisherman, A Catch Worthy of Njord, The Blacksmith, The Artisan, The Master Chef, The Gourmet, The Chosen One, Mighty Halls, Top of the World, The Carpenter, The Explorer, The Sailor, The Captain, Kings and Queens of Valheim, Child of Odin and That Can't Be an Accident. The game only says you've discovered a biome, and awards an achievement, once for each character, whatever the world - so a character who found the Swamp in another world isn't news for it here
- A kill is news to every player who hit the creature, and a tree's to the player who felled it - or who last hit its log, which sent it rolling. A tame is news to the player nearest the animal, and the rest to the player they happened to. Nothing done by cheating or with console commands is news - a creature the game marks as killed by someone cheating, the console's kill and tame commands, or a skill raised with `raiseskill`
- A Viking tells you your news when they greet you or you talk to them - 90% of the time, only the latest they haven't told you, and only news from after they last talked to you, so a Viking you've just met has none for you, and talking to a Viking again right after has them chat as usual. A Viking who doesn't know you yet only greets you. The Vikings who tell an event each say a different line about it, and once 2-4 of them have, it's no longer news
- A boss killed again takes the place of its news, told to everyone who has killed it while it was news, and a death, a leviathan, a sea serpent or a monster killed with a tree takes the place of your last news of that kind. News is forgotten after 10 game days, told or not
- News of a boss or biome beyond a Viking's own village's biome - the Plains' Yagluth to a Mountain village, say - has them add how impressed they are
- The server keeps the news, saved with the world, and every player's game gets it from the server, so the Vikings of every village know it
- The villages hear from each other too: when a village is disbanded or started, a quest comes home with something worth talking about, a Viking dies or a newcomer arrives, the villages within 3 km hear about it for 3 game days - by rune stick or raven, or from a Viking who came from there, who brought the news themselves. Now and then a Viking tells you, or talks it over with another - a village being disbanded most of all, and a Viking who came from a village most of all about that one

## Dvergr Vikings

Some Vikings have taken work with the Dvergr in the Mistlands. A Dvergr Viking is a living Viking in the Dvergr's pay, who keeps to their outposts and patrols and never joins a village.

- Each of the Dvergr's outposts hires some, once: the first time you've been within it for 5 seconds with its Dvergr up, it hires anywhere from none to a quarter of the Dvergr still alive there, rounded to the nearest, each number as likely, up to 4 - none with 1 Dvergr, up to 1 with 2-5, up to 2 with 6-9, and so on. They appear beside its Dvergr, keeping to their post when the Dvergr beside them do. An outpost never hires again, whether they live or die, and one whose Dvergr were killed before the mod came along hires none
- Half of the Dvergr patrols the world spawns have one along, who wanders with them
- They're on the Dvergr's side - the Dvergr, Dvergr Vikings and living Vikings never hurt each other. Like the Dvergr, they can be hit with PvP off, and the console's kill commands kill them. Hurt one, or a Dvergr near one, and every Dvergr, Dvergr Viking and living Viking within 20 meters turns on you - and like the Dvergr, a Dvergr Viking stays angry
- They fight like zombie Vikings: the health and damage of a Mistlands zombie Viking, one star as often as the game gives the creatures it spawns there, and a pause between swings - but their armor protects them in full
- Like living Vikings, they have the Mistlands' gear, fully repaired, with a Dverger circlet they light at night and a wisp light against the mist, and a name of their own - they're plain Viking to you until they know you. Their skills rise as they use them, and they see no farther in the mist than living Vikings, but for the wisp light they carry
- They have words of their own, about working for the Dvergr and how they came to be hired - greeting you as you arrive and when you talk to them, calling out the enemies they go after, shouting for help when they're badly hurt, and passing on your news the way living Vikings do, sharing only the living Vikings' lines about the Mistlands, about old friends and how you're doing, and about the runestones where they stand and of someone with their name (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)). They stand facing you for 4 seconds when you talk to them, and remember when they met you and how many times you've talked, the way living Vikings do
- You can talk to them, but can't name them or open their inventory
- They know the forsaken powers up to Yagluth's, learn the ones they're given, and use them in fights and when badly hurt (see Forsaken Powers)
- When they die they drop 100-140 coins and 0-2 entrails, multiplied by their stars, and each piece of their gear has a chance to drop (see Configuration)

## Forsaken Powers

Living Vikings know the powers of their biome and every biome before it, learn the powers they're given, use them in fights and for their work, and teach them to each other at dusk (see Village Life).

- A power a living or Dvergr Viking uses reaches you and the other players they aren't angry at, and the Dvergr, Dvergr Vikings and living Vikings within 10 meters who aren't angry at players
- Your own power reaches the living Vikings, Dvergr Vikings and Dvergr within 10 meters who aren't angry at you

## With Zombie Apocalypse

With [Zombie Apocalypse](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Kriona/ZombieApocalypse/) installed too, the living Vikings live through its apocalypse:

- A living or Dvergr Viking who dies rises as a zombie Viking with their looks, gear and skills, as any slain creature does under Zombie Apocalypse's rules, and drops coins like any zombie Viking when killed in turn - along with the wood and loot they carried for their village
- They stand ready over the corpses about to rise, fight the zombie once it's up rather than a corpse that can't be hurt, and keep clear of the poison clouds zombies' corpses leave (see Village Life)
- They talk of the dead walking (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)) - of burning the dead only when Zombie Apocalypse's `FireStaysDead` lets fire keep them down, and of silver whenever they do, since spirit damage always keeps the dead down
- The empty abandoned houses near a farm are its Vikings', so zombie Vikings never hold them
- A name with Zombie Apocalypse's zombie word in it is one they turn down

Any version of Viking Villages works with any version of Zombie Apocalypse - what one has that the other doesn't know about yet is left out.

## Configuration

`BepInEx\config\kriona.VikingVillages.cfg` is created on first run, and changes take effect as soon as the file is saved. You do not need to restart Valheim or rejoin the world.

In multiplayer, every player uses the server's settings while connected to it, and gets its changes as soon as they're saved. A player's own settings come back when they leave the server.

Viking Gear:

- `HelmetChance` - percent chance that a living Viking also wears their armor set's helmet - from the Mountains on, every one wears one (default `50`)
- `CapeChance` - percent chance that a living Viking also wears their armor set's cape - from the Mountains on, every one wears one (default `50`)
- `ShieldChance` - percent chance that a living Viking carries a shield, unless their weapon takes both hands (default `40`)

Dvergr Vikings:

- `GearDropChance` - percent chance that a Dvergr Viking drops each piece of their gear when they die (default `5`)
- `GearDropMax` - most pieces of gear a Dvergr Viking drops (default `2`)

To leave the villages abandoned, uninstall the mod.

## Console Commands

- `kri_vv_spawnvillage [farm]` - needs `devcommands`, and only works in single player or for the host, since only the server places locations. Spawns an abandoned farm centered where you stand - the one named, or one picked at random - and moves you out past where its fence can stand, facing it, so you aren't caught in a house or the bonfire as it goes up. Its village is built and set up over the next few seconds, the same as one found in the world, and it's added to the world's plan of its locations unless its zone already has one
- `kri_vv_blueprints [reload]` - lists the house blueprints villages are built with and any problems with them, reading them again first with `reload`
- `kri_vv_serverrun [start|stop]` - an experiment, which needs `devcommands` and only works on a dedicated server. `start`, standing in a village, has the server run that village whenever no player is within 100 meters of it, so its Vikings carry on with nobody there, and starts a test run on the server, saved in the server's own `BepInEx\VikingVillagesTestLog\`. When a player comes near, the server lets go and their game takes over the village. `stop` ends it. It runs one village at a time, and lasts until it's stopped or the server shuts down. On its own, the command says how the run is going
- `kri_vv_testlog [start|stop]` - for long tests of a village getting by on its own. `start` begins a test run of the village you're in or near - or, with no village near, of the first one set up near you, so its building and setup are in the run too, from an unexplored farm or `kri_vv_spawnvillage` - writing extra lines to the log: the time on every line, why Vikings die, where they get into trouble, loot that despawns before they put it away, a snapshot of every village and Viking every 15 minutes with totals of what they did so far, and the final totals when the run ends. `stop` ends it, and so does leaving the world or closing the game. Everything Viking Villages logs during the run goes to `BepInEx\LogOutput.log` as usual, and to the run's own file in `BepInEx\VikingVillagesTestLog\`, named after the village's biome, like `Meadows.log`, then `Meadows-2.log` for the next run there, so no earlier run is written over. The totals count the quests that set out, came back or were given up on, what they brought home, and the ore and fuel put in smelters. On its own, the command says how the run is going
  - `kri_vv_testlog quest` shows everything behind the quests of the village you're in - its goal and the next thing it does toward it, the quest it's on, what each Viking on it takes, works with and brings home, whether its food and wood are running out, its treasure, whether it's trapped, and the roads to quests it knows
  - `kri_vv_testlog quest <kind> [item]` starts a quest of that kind at once, whatever the village wants or the time of day - `kri_vv_testlog quest MineCopper` or `kri_vv_testlog quest ChopTrees FineWood`, say, with the kinds listed when the one given isn't one - as long as the village can go on it and spare the Vikings. The first time for a quest, it asks the server for the road and has you run it again
  - `kri_vv_testlog quest due` brings the village's quest home now, once its Vikings have left - only for the host, whose game keeps them
  - Each snapshot also lists the groups of Vikings kept on the road - where they're going, roughly where they are now, when they're due and what they can do on the road - and the news the villages have heard of each other, and the log notes every road planned, every group taken off the road, rerouted or due, and every boat built, boarded and broken up
- `kri_vv_travel <village name|quest kind|random> [count] [sim]` - needs `devcommands`. Sends the living Viking you're looking at, or else `count` Vikings of the village you're in (1 by default, nearest you first), off to the village named - any village whose name holds what you typed, whatever its biome - or one picked at random, as a group. They leave their village and travel the way any group on the road does, in the world while you're near, so you can follow them, or straight off the world with `sim`. A quest kind, like `MineCopper`, starts the village's quest of that kind at once, the same as `kri_vv_testlog quest`
- `kri_vv_spawnboat <Raft|Karve|VikingShip|VikingShip_Ashlands> [full|max|vikings]` - needs `devcommands`. Spawns one of the boats the Vikings build, by its prefab ID - or by its name, like `longship` or `drakkar` - moored on the first deep water in front of you, with new living Vikings already sitting in its seats - a full crew with `full`, `max` or nothing given, or as many as given, up to a full crew - to see where they all sit. The boat never sets off, and they sit there doing nothing else until it's broken, when they swim ashore and go their way like any Viking with no village. It drops nothing when it breaks. `kri_vv_spawnboat despawn` takes away every boat spawned with it within 150 meters of you, with the Vikings still on board them - leaving the boats Vikings build on the road alone, and the Vikings of a boat that broke, who are on their way by then. `kri_vv_spawnboat pos` says where you stand on the boat you're on - or the nearest spawned with the command within 30 meters - in meters from the ship's middle across, up and along it, the way the seats are laid out, with the nearest seat, also written to the log - `kri_vv_spawnboat pos copy` copies it to the clipboard too

## Installation

With a mod manager, install Viking Villages from [Thunderstore](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Kriona/VikingVillages/) using r2modman or the Thunderstore Mod Manager (which install BepInExPack for Valheim).

By hand:

1. Install BepInExPack for Valheim from [Thunderstore](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/denikson/BepInExPack_Valheim/) or [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/valheim/mods/3605)
2. Extract the zip into your Valheim folder, or copy `VikingVillages.dll` and its `Blueprints` folder into `BepInEx\plugins\VikingVillages`

## Multiplayer

In multiplayer, the server and every player need the same version of the mod. A server with it turns away a player without it, or with another version, and says why. On a server without it, the mod stays off and the player can still join - and its console commands don't work there, and its Vikings and village anchors can't be spawned there, since no one else would have them.
