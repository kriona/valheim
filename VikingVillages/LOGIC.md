## Villages

### Farms

- Each farm is generated at its own size, picked at random, so villages come in a range of sizes - its fence anywhere from 24 to 44 meters from its middle, where the game's is 18-32, and from 36 to 44 meters for a farm in or within 150 meters of any biome harder than the Meadows, like the Black Forest - so its houses, fields and pens spread out in a large one, and the trees, bushes, rocks and logs within 6 meters past its fence are cleared away - branches, stones, berries and the like are left. Oaks are never cleared: the village is built around them, its houses at least 7 meters from an oak's trunk to keep their roofs out of its branches, its fields, pens and fence at least 3 meters, both farther for a bigger oak, and its paths and flattened ground go around the trunk. Its bonfire stands on open ground in its very middle, with no building within 7 meters of it, its farmhouses around it, within 45% of the fence's distance from the middle or 15 meters, whichever is farther, and its pens out toward the fence, past 65% of that distance - its fields and the rest go anywhere else. It gets as many more fields, pens and the rest as it takes in more ground, so it's as full as the game's own, but no more farmhouses
- Every village has at least 3 houses in its core, or 4 for a farm in or within 150 meters of any biome harder than the Meadows, like the Black Forest - a farm generated with fewer, counting its farmhouses and the empty houses close by them, gets more farmhouses inside its fence, or just outside it when there's no room inside
- No village stands over the world's start, where you first arrive - a farm the game would put within 68 meters of the start's edge, close enough for its village to reach it, is left out of the world, which then has one fewer farm, and a village a portal leads to is never put there either
- Two villages whose farms are within 100 meters of each other are joined by a path between their nearest farmhouses
- A village counts as the hardest biome within 50 meters of its farm's middle - from the Meadows through the Black Forest, the Swamp, the Mountains, the Plains, the Mistlands and the Ashlands to the Deep North - so a farm in the Meadows by the Black Forest is a Black Forest village through and through. Everything about a village goes by that one biome - its Vikings, wherever around it they appear, its stations and their upgrades, starter food and pickaxes, cooking stations, tools, cart, shore boat, raised ground, bridges, wisp lights, portal home, snow clearing and quests (see [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md#which-biome-counts))

### Houses, Core and Outposts

- A village's core is its farmhouses and the empty houses close by them - within 15 meters, wall to wall, of a house already in the core. Its Vikings live only there. The empty houses standing off on their own, or only near each other, are outposts - fixed up and furnished, but no one's home, and the Vikings only go out to them by day, to repair them, put loot away or use their chests. A farm's village reaches 2 meters past its fence, taking in its pens and fields along with its houses (or 8 meters past the walls of its core houses when one stands farther out, or on a farm generated before the mod noted its fence's size), and, separately, 8 meters past the walls of each outpost or group of outposts close together, so the open ground between the core and an outpost off on its own isn't the village's - an enemy out there isn't in the village, and loot lying there isn't the village's to pick up. A village you started counts its bonfire and the houses close by it as its core
- Half of a farm's farmhouses, picked at random, are built from the house designs in the mod's `Blueprints` folder instead, when it has any - PlanBuild `.blueprint` files, which PlanBuild and Infinity Hammer both save. Each stands in the farmhouses' ring on ground that rises and falls no more than a meter under it, its doors turned toward the bonfire, and keeps the furniture it was designed with rather than being furnished. A design with no door isn't used. The `kri_vv_blueprints` console command lists the designs and anything wrong with them, and `kri_vv_blueprints reload` reads them again, for farms generated after
- An abandoned house with no creatures of its own within 100 meters of a farm is taken in by its Vikings, and fixed up the same way, with its vines cleared away. One in the village's core is home to 2 more Vikings, who move in along with the farmhouses' Vikings. A village generated when its houses held 1-2 Vikings each is short the Vikings it now holds, and gets them back over the days the way it gets back its dead

### Moving In and Upkeep

- Each farmhouse of a farm is home to 2 living Vikings, never starred, who move in once its bonfire is built, the first time the whole village has loaded around you - all at once, in the village beside its houses, whether you can see them or not
- The Vikings keep their village in repair - none of its walls, floors or roofs start out damaged, and none of its roof tiles, wall tops or doors are missing
- Nothing of it falls down for want of support on uneven ground: a fence section left floating is lowered onto the ground, or taken away when the ground falls more than 2 meters under it, and a house's floor, wall, stair or pole that reaches neither the ground nor a piece under it gets a column of stone blocks under it, down into the ground - put right once its paths are laid, with nothing of the farm wearing until then
- In the Deep North, the deep snow on its ground is cleared as its paths are laid - within 8 meters of its fence and its houses, outposts included - and the snow its pieces start out buried under is knocked off; after that, the Vikings clear off the snow that builds up on its pieces and any deep snow left on its ground (see [Clear Snow](JOBS.md#clear-snow))
- A farmhouse that would have had a wild beehive hanging inside, stinging the Vikings, has a beehive for honey standing outside instead

### Furnishing

- The houses are furnished the first time the whole village has loaded around you - in each, a wood bed for every Viking who calls it home (so none in an outpost), a wood chest or two, and a wood table with 2-4 stools around it. Beds, chests, the workbench and the forge stand against the walls, the table in the open middle. Only what fits goes in - the way from the door to every piece stays clear and at least a meter and a half wide, since a narrower gap between two pieces is too tight for a Viking to walk through, with a gap at least a meter deep beside each to stand in, so does the way to every bed and chest the house already had, and at least half the floor stays free, so a small house gets fewer pieces, and a Viking without a bed sits on a stool or the floor at night. The Vikings repair and rebuild the furniture like the rest of their village
- Any chest a farm or a house it takes in is generated with is the Vikings' own too, starting out empty rather than full of treasure, while a buried treasure chest stays the world's. The village's chests start out with a full stack each of wood, stone and resin, 10 each of two of its biome's foods - like cooked boar meat and raspberries in the Meadows - so the Vikings have something to eat before they forage, hunt and cook their own, and outside the Meadows 2 pickaxes of its biome - antler in the Black Forest, bronze in the Swamp, iron in the Mountains and the Plains, and black metal from the Mistlands on - each in a chest picked at random
- The village gets a workbench and a forge in its core houses - each in a different house when there are enough - unless one already stands somewhere in the village, whoever built it. Every village has both: one missing either - with no room for it when it was furnished, or a core of houses built from blueprints - gets it right then, or the first time an older village loads, and only then, so none appears later on: in a house of its core with room for it, or else in one built from a blueprint, or else out on flat open ground in the village, away from its fire, under a little shelter of its own - a wall behind it and at each end and a wood roof, open at the front - which covers it enough to use. With no flat ground for it, the ground is flattened for it, clearing the trees (but never an oak), logs, bushes and rocks in the way, and when even that leaves nowhere, the shelter stands on a wooden platform at the flattest spot - floors with room to stand in front, posts down to the ground and stairs down from the front. Houses and spots out of your sight are tried first
- The village's own workbench and forge, and any black forge or galdr table its houses came with, are then upgraded far enough to repair the gear of its biome and every biome before it: a level 2 workbench in the Meadows and level 3 from the Black Forest on, a forge of level 2 in the Swamp, 3 in the Mountains and 4 from the Plains on, a level 4 black forge from the Ashlands on, and a galdr table of level 2 in the Ashlands and 4 in the Deep North. The upgrades a station is missing go within its reach, counting any it already has: against a wall of its house, as close as fits, or on level ground beside its shelter, keeping the floor in front of every station clear. An older village gets them the first time it loads, and only then. The Vikings, like you, can only use a workbench or forge with a roof over it
- A bed the houses came with that's only for show, which you can't use or claim, is swapped for a real one where it stands the first time the village loads - the game's bed of the same kind when there is one, like a dragon bed for a prop dragon bed, or else a wood bed

### Ground, Paths and Bridges

- The ground around the farm and its houses is flattened the first time the whole village has loaded around you - its bumps smoothed out and its slopes eased, leaving the ground right around buildings as it is, with trees, rocks and bushes moved up or down with the ground. Dry ground is never lowered into the water - it stops half a meter above it - so a village on low ground, like the Plains by the sea, doesn't flood. A village in the Swamp or the Plains has its ground raised out of any water it stands in, up to half a meter above it - fully within 30 meters of its middle and 10 of its houses, sloping back down into the swamp by 44 and 20 meters - by at most 3 meters, leaving the ground right around buildings as it is, and its paths cross the raised ground rather than bridge it
- Dirt paths join the farmhouses and the houses near the farm, right up to their doors, laid the first time the whole village has loaded around you. They wind around buildings, fences, large trees, bushes and large rocks, keep to flat ground, and clear away the branches, stones, small trees and small rocks in their way. Where there's no way around, a path goes through as little of what's in its way as it can, so every house is joined. The ground under the houses' floors is painted the same way, so grass doesn't grow through them
- A path crosses a river or stream where it's narrow, on a bridge resting on both banks, and goes around water too wide to bridge - villages whose paths were laid before bridges get theirs the next time they load. A bridge is of wood floors, up to 14 meters across, or in the Ashlands and the Deep North of grausten floors, which hold each other up over a shorter span than wood, so a grausten bridge reaches only as far as they do
- In the Ashlands, the lava and the boiling sea count as water - the paths go around them, crossing only on a bridge, and the village's bonfire and portal never stand on them

### The Fire

- A bonfire stands on the open ground nearest the middle of the farm, clear of its buildings and paths, built along with the paths and stacked with as much wood as it holds. Sloping ground is flattened for it. A farm with no room for a bonfire within 30 meters of its middle stays an abandoned farm - no fire, paths, bridges, flattened ground or furniture, and no living Vikings ever move in
- While it burns it pushes back the fog - the nearer you are to it, the thinner the weather's fog, clearing it fully within 15 meters and back to all of it 45 meters out, thinning and thickening over a few seconds as the fire lights or goes out - except in the Mistlands, whose mist a fire doesn't touch
- The village's fire crackles and roars at half the game's loudness, since it burns all night beside where the Vikings sleep - a bonfire or campfire you build that they take up as theirs too, and it's back to full once it's no longer theirs
- Once the fire is lit, the village gets cooking stations over it - two back to back across a bonfire, or one over a campfire - each as far out from the fire's middle as it can stand and still cook. A village from the Swamp on gets iron cooking stations, which also cook what the Swamp's and later biomes' hunts bring in. A village's lit bonfire runs any cooking station sitting on it - yours too - as long as part of the station is in its flames, even one that would otherwise need a hearth or several campfires under it. A village with a campfire gets a wooden station when an iron one won't fit over it
- A village from the Black Forest on also gets a cauldron in the flames beside them, as far out as it still stands in them, so it's used from outside them, with more of the cauldron's extensions around it for each biome after the Black Forest - the spice rack for the Swamp, then the butcher's table, the pots and pans, the mortar and pestle, the rolling pins, and the smoker for the Deep North. Each faces the cauldron at the nearest spot from 2.5 meters out, past where the cauldron is used from, to half a meter inside its reach of it, with all of it out of the flames and nothing in its way
- Two sitting logs go around the fire too, facing it, where there's room for them - on dry, fairly flat ground with nothing in the way, outside the houses and off the paths, within the fire's warmth and spread around it. The Vikings rebuild them like the rest of their village
- When the village's bonfire or campfire is destroyed, a Viking builds it back where it stood with nothing - the fire drops nothing when it breaks or is taken down, either. If you build a bonfire or campfire within 6 meters of where it stood first, the Vikings take that up as their fire instead - gathering at it, cooking over it, keeping it stacked and building it back where it stands if it's destroyed - and it still drops what you built it from. Once it's lit, the village's cooking stations move over it: the ones that don't cook over it are no longer built back, those the Vikings built that stand empty are taken down, and new ones are placed over the new fire. Their sitting logs move too: a Viking takes each log down with their hammer, keeping its wood, and builds it back around the new fire - unless you've built a sitting log around it yourself, when theirs stay where they are
- A village's bonfire is never built in a pen, and when an older one stands in one, the Vikings gather at their houses at dusk instead

### Boats

- A farm whose edge comes within 24 meters of water that runs on to the sea - the sea itself, or a lake or river joined to it - gets a boat moored on it the first time the whole village has loaded around you: the best boat of the village's biome, the same as its Vikings would build (a raft in the Meadows), as near the village as there's water deep enough all around it - up to 80 meters past its edge - facing along the most open water. It's yours to take - the Vikings never build it back - and breaking it drops a third of what it's built from, like any piece no player built. A village on the shore can also get a boat from Vikings who sail there, when it has none of that kind (see [Sailing](#sailing)). Its Vikings sail its boats on a quest that sets out by sea, and bring them home again (see [Sailing From the Village](QUESTS.md#sailing-from-the-village))

### The Cart

- Every village from the Black Forest on has a cart, but not in the Ashlands. Only Vikings pull it or open it. Like their boats, they build it from nothing, and it drops nothing it's made of when it breaks or is taken down, only what it held. A farm's village gets one parked outside beside a house of its core the first time the whole village has loaded around you - on the side of the house away from its door, or else to the door's left or right, never on the side the door is on, and at least 2.5 meters past the cart's own length from every door and doorstep in the village, on fairly flat ground with nothing in the way, off the paths where it can and away from the fire. A village you started gets none, and its Vikings build their own
- A quest that would bring home more than its Vikings can carry takes the cart along, the first of them pulling it out of the village and home again full, and the Vikings put away what it holds (see [Taking the Cart](QUESTS.md#taking-the-cart) in QUESTS.md)
- When it ends up outside the village, a Viking walks out to it, hooks it up and pulls it home, parking it beside a house nearest where it stood, rather than where it was before
- When it's destroyed, or has been out of reach for a game day, a Viking builds another beside a house with one swing of a hammer. Should the old one turn up again within 40 meters of the village, a Viking takes it down, which drops whatever it held for the Vikings to put away (see [Look After the Cart](JOBS.md#look-after-the-cart) in JOBS.md)

### Signs

- The farm's blank signposts are signs with a name on each, picked at random from these and from the names the world's other villages go by, wherever they are - though only one of a village's signs points to another village. A sign with a wooden post through it is moved off the post, facing away from it, so its text isn't cut off. You can write your own over it, like any sign, and a sign the Vikings build back gets a new name
  - Realms: Valhalla, Asgard, Midgard, Helheim, Jotunheim, Niflheim, Bifrost, Yggdrasil
  - Bosses: Eikthyr, Elder
  - Biomes: Black Forest, Swamp, Plains, Mistlands, Deep North
  - Places: Mead Hall, Burial Chambers, Troll Cave

### Portals Home

- A village of the Ashlands or the Deep North comes with a portal home, put up with its paths: a stone portal on open ground out past the houses nearest its middle, at least 16 meters from it, joined to a wood portal out the same way in a village of the Plains or an easier biome. The other village is one of the world's farms no one has been near yet, picked at random - or, with none left, a new farm in a part of the world no one has been to, or, with the whole world explored, a new farm somewhere with nothing anyone built and no other location within 150 meters. Its farm is generated and its village named right away, so you can go through before anyone has been there - its portal waits in the middle of the farm until the village loads around someone, then moves out to its own open ground
- Both portals are named after the Ashlands or Deep North village - cut short to 10 characters, as many as you can type for a portal, with #2, #3 and so on after the name when another portal has it already - each has a dirt path to the nearest of its village's paths and a sign on its frame naming the village on the other side, and neither stands on lava. When there's nowhere left for another village, the Ashlands or Deep North village has no portal
- Each portal and its sign are the village's own, like the pieces it was generated with: they break into what they're built of, and the Vikings build them back, linked to the same village, with that village's name on the sign as it goes by now - though only while that village is still there and wasn't abandoned for want of room for its bonfire, and the sign only once its portal stands
- The Vikings of both villages talk about the village through their portal and its biome, among themselves and now and then to you

### Lights Against the Mist

- A village of the Mistlands is lit against the mist, put up with its paths: a wisp fountain, drawing wisps you can pick, on open ground at least 8 meters from its fire, and wisp torches - standing torches that push back the mist - 4 around the fire 8 meters out, one every 14 meters around the inside of its fence, and more along its paths wherever a path runs more than 10 meters from any light, each on open ground beside the path. They're the village's own, like the pieces it was generated with: they break into what they're built of, and the Vikings build back those within the village

### Livestock

- The boars in a farm's pens are the Vikings' livestock, tamed as they spawn while a living Viking is within 40 meters, or within a second afterwards while the village has living Vikings - they leave the Vikings and you alone, and fight off zombies and monsters that come within 12 meters of where they spawned, standing their ground rather than running from them as a wild boar would, even by a fire. One a Viking slaughters, or hits by accident, neither runs nor fights back. The Vikings only go for one when they're slaughtering it - for what they need to craft with and nothing wild drops, or for its meat when the chests hold fewer than three foods for each Viking and there's no wild prey about that drops food (see [JOBS.md](JOBS.md#slaughter)) - even a Viking angry at you leaves the rest alone. Wild boars that wander into the village stay wild
- They're the Vikings' to keep, so you can't pet, rename or breed them, and looking at one shows its village's name after its own. Once the farm's Vikings are gone, its boars spawn wild. A pen whose boar is killed gets a new one once its spawner's wait is up - 20 minutes for one the game gives no wait, which would never spawn again - while you're nearby, even inside the village, where a fire or a bed would keep the game's spawners from spawning

### Who Runs a Village

- While you're in a village, your game runs everything in it and up to 80 meters past its edge - its Vikings, the monsters they fight, its animals, fires and dropped items - wherever it is, as long as it's loaded around you. The game itself only runs what's within 64 to 128 meters of you, depending on where you stand, so without this a Viking working at an outpost past that would stand frozen there with the monster they were fighting until you came closer. In multiplayer, each player runs the village they're in, and a player already running something keeps it

### Village Rings

- Everywhere inside a village's rings is a player base, like a workbench's area: the game's random spawns and creature spawners don't spawn there, and items dropped there don't despawn. Each ring also counts toward your base value while you're within 20 meters of it, as a workbench or fire does, so the game's raids, which need a base value of 3, can come to a village sooner
- A village reaches over what it has built and the paths between: a farm's whole fenced ground, pens and fields included, 8 meters past the walls of each of its houses and around each of its own pieces - its fences, cooking stations, sitting logs, beehives and the rest - and 4 meters either side of its paths, following them as they wind - read from the path painted on the ground, whether the farm, a Viking or you dug it, every 30 seconds while the village is loaded. A path only counts when it leads from the village back into it, to a house off on its own, say, rather than coming to a dead end or running off to somewhere else. Open ground off to the side of it, between an outpost and the rest, isn't in the village
- Press V while looking at a village's bonfire or campfire to show how far the village reaches, as green rings on the ground like a workbench's, without its floating hammer, their marks all creeping around at the same pace whatever the ring's size, with rings that overlap shown as one outline around them all, rather than crossing each other - and they grow and shrink with the village. Press V again to hide them. Only you see them, and they stay shown for you until you hide them - when you walk away and come back, and when you log off and on again. The village keeps track of who has them shown, so your character's own data doesn't fill up with villages. At a fire where V does this, makes a gathering fire or starts a village, it doesn't also toggle auto-pickup, which V does by default

### Village Names

- Every village comes with a name of its own once its paths are laid - a Norse town or place of the Viking age like Hedeby, Jomsborg, Kaupang or Uppsala, or one like Frostfjord, Ravenfell, Grimsby, Langtoft or Stormnes, among about 250 (see [NAMES.md](NAMES.md)) - one no other village in the world has, so its Home pin goes on your map as soon as it loads around you. The names are settled in advance for every farm the world was made with, found or not, so each farm's name stays the same however the world is explored; a village a player starts takes one no farm has. A village you've already named keeps the name you gave it
- Rename a village: look at its bonfire or campfire and press N, then type a name of up to 24 characters. Its Vikings won't have it go without a name, be called just Village, or be named after a god, giant or beast of the myths, a boss or someone you meet like Odin, Fenrir, Eikthyr or Haldor, a legendary weapon, a biome or a place from the lore, or with a text tag like `<color=red>` in it, much as they turn down for themselves - the one nearest you within 30 meters says so, or with none that near, it's said on screen - and it keeps the name it had. Any village can be named, a farm's or one you started, as long as a ward lets you in. The Viking of the village nearest you within 30 meters has something to say about it as you name or rename it
- The name shows in a banner as you give it, over the fire in orange while you're within 20 meters of it - the way a creature's name shows over it - on the fire when you look at it, and in what its Vikings say - their greetings, alarms, talks and goodbyes. A Home pin with the name goes on your map where the village stands, and on every player's map once the village loads around them - moved over to the new name when it's renamed. Take the pin off yourself and it stays off until the village is renamed. A village you disband keeps its name at its bonfire, and has it back when you start it there again
- While you're inside a named village's rings, its name shows at the top of the screen, looking the same as it does over its fire. With aedenthorn's [Clock Mod](https://www.nexusmods.com/valheim/mods/85) installed, the name takes the clock's spot and the clock moves down under it until you leave - without changing where Clock Mod saves the clock. Without it, the name shows at the top middle of the screen, where that clock would be

### Village Stock

- Press I while looking at a village's bonfire or campfire to see the village's stock - everything in its chests, the ones its Vikings use, in one place beside your inventory, the way a chest opens - a slot for every slot of every chest, each stack as it lies in its chest, sorted by kind, with the empty slots after them, so you can see how much room the village has left. It's to look at only: nothing can be taken out or put in, and it shows no weight. Point at a stack and a gold arrow shows over the chest it's in, with the item's name and how many of it that chest holds over the arrow - pinned to the edge of the stock's window, pointing in, for a chest the window hides, and to the edge of the screen for one that's out of sight. It keeps up as the Vikings move things about, and closes when you press I again or walk away from the fire

### Village Chests

- Every chest in the village's rings or inside one of its houses that's open to everyone is the village's own, whoever built it - a chest you build there included, inside a ward or not. The Vikings put loot and wood in it, take food, gear and tools from it, count its room before building another chest (see [Loot and Chests](#loot-and-chests)), and empty it when the village is disbanded (see [Starting and Disbanding a Village](#starting-and-disbanding-a-village)). Its contents show in the village's stock
- Press V while looking at one to keep the Vikings out of it, with the sound of a ward turning on, and press V again to let them use it again, with the sound of a ward turning off. A chest they're kept out of is left alone - they never put anything in it or take anything out, it doesn't count toward their room or show in the stock, and disbanding leaves it as it is. This works on any of the village's chests, the ones it was furnished with or its Vikings built too. A ward keeps players it doesn't permit from changing it

### Gathering Fires

- Press V while looking at any other fire in a village that gives warmth and stands outside its houses - a bonfire, campfire or the like you built - to make it a gathering fire, so a large village spreads out over several fires at night. The Vikings gather and rest at it, keep it fueled and use the seats around it like their own fire's, but they don't build it back, or put sitting logs or cooking stations around it. Press V again to make it an ordinary fire. A ward keeps players it doesn't permit from changing it

### Beds and Growing the Village

- A bed you build in a village, or within 30 meters of its edge, and leave unclaimed brings the village a new Viking right away - as long as it's under a roof and covered enough that you could claim it. Claim it as soon as you build it if it's for you. The Viking walks in from outside the fence the same way one coming back does - with no hostile creature in the village or within 10 meters of it, or within 20 meters of where it comes from - and calls the house the bed is in home, or else the nearest house of the village's core. The Vikings sleep in that bed wherever it is in the village. The bed keeps its Viking while it stands unclaimed - once it's gone or you claim it, the village is back to the Vikings it had, so the extra Viking isn't brought back when they die
- A bed in a building of your own grows the village to take the building in - its walls are measured from the bed, and the village reaches out to it, so keep building houses with beds and the village keeps growing. A building within 15 meters of the village's core, wall to wall, joins the core and the Vikings live there - farther out, it's an outpost they visit by day. Once it's joined, a Viking makes a dirt path from its door to the nearest house of the village with a hoe, the way you would - steering around what the village's own paths steer around and clearing the branches, stones, small trees and small rocks on it (see [Make Paths](JOBS.md#make-paths)). Once none of its beds that brought a Viking is left standing unclaimed, the village shrinks back. The Vikings repair your buildings in it, but don't rebuild them

### Starting and Disbanding a Village

- Start a village of your own: look at a bonfire you built, press V and say yes when asked. It has to be more than 60 meters from any other village's edge and 100 meters from any farm, and a ward has to let you in. The game's banner and fanfare for finding a new land mark it, and the first time, Hugin flies in to tell you what the village needs. It starts with no Vikings - each bed you build within 30 meters of it and leave unclaimed brings one, the same as in any village, and the village grows to take in the building the bed is in, reaching over the bonfire as well as its houses. Its Vikings live there like any village's - they gather at the bonfire, keep it fueled, cook over it, repair what you build, keep watch and come back when they die - but nothing is built or furnished for them: no paths, furniture, chests, workbench or forge, so build what they need. If the bonfire is destroyed, they build it back
- To disband it, press Shift+V at its bonfire and say yes - its Vikings drop whatever they were doing and gather at the bonfire, talking about the village as they come, for up to 4 minutes or until they're all there. Once they know where they're going, they first empty the village's own chests within 60 meters of the fire - every chest they used when it was disbanded, yours included unless you kept them out of it (see [Village Chests](#village-chests)) - taking what they can carry without being slowed, food first, then wood and stone, then everything else, and trophies last. When the chests hold more than that, each takes a cart: the village's own when it stands empty near the fire, or else one they build beside the fire from nothing, one cart each at most, which they fill an armful at a time up to its full load of 800 weight, putting back what doesn't fit. A Meadows or Ashlands village's Vikings take no cart, and a cart isn't taken or built when everything fits in their packs. Vikings disbanded with no player near, who go straight onto the road, leave the chests as they are. They stay there 20-30 seconds more, wondering where to go now, until one of them says which village they're going to - the one they can all get to soonest (see [Vikings With No Village](#vikings-with-no-village)) - and 2 seconds later they set out together with everything they have, each one within 20 meters of you turning to wave and say goodbye first. Those farther off than anyone can see set out for it at once. With no village they can reach, they leave the way they always have: each one within 20 meters of you turns to you, waves and says goodbye, a few seconds apart, and then they run off the way night monsters do at dawn - away from the nearest player within 40 meters, fighting whatever comes after them on the way, until no one is that close and they're gone. The beds that brought them are free again, and the bonfire stays as an ordinary fire
- Start a village at that bonfire again and every Viking who hasn't settled in another village yet comes back, as they were when they left - their name, gear, inventory and skills, and the players they know - one to each unclaimed bed near it, as the beds call for Vikings, before any new Viking comes. Those still gathering or near enough to see stop and turn back - one near you says so - and walk home, each taking the first unclaimed bed that calls for a Viking, ahead of those coming back from farther off. Those already farther off go on to the village they were going to, say so when they get there, and turn around for home - a message says how many are on their way and roughly when, in game time, like "3 Vikings are on the way back - expect them in a couple of hours". One coming home to a village with no bed for them waits there 5 minutes, then comes to its beds like the rest. Those who left with no village to go to come back right away. Destroy the bonfire, and those not on the road are gone for good
- Disband a village while its Vikings are out on a quest and they go on to the village the rest of its Vikings went to once they're done, as much later as the road from the village disbanded takes - or with no village found, to whichever village they can reach. Start a village at its bonfire again and they still go on there first, then turn around for home

## Living Vikings

### Who They Are

- A living Viking is a Viking of their village's biome - their skills rolled from their biome's range, from 1-12 in the Meadows to 88-100 in the Deep North, each skill rolled on its own, their health what their food gives them, never below their biome's least (see [Eating](#eating)), and their melee hits scaled by their biome (see [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md)) - and their gear all their biome's own (a piece their biome has none of, like a bow, still comes from the nearest biome below that has one), and from the Mountains on they always wear their set's helmet
- They don't groan like the draugr
- Spawn one with the console command `spawn Viking` - near a village they join it, and out in the wild they're lost for a little while before setting out for the nearest village (see [Vikings With No Village](#vikings-with-no-village))

### Skills

- A living Viking starts with their own level in each skill, rolled within their biome's range - every Viking is at least level 1 in every skill - and their skills rise the way yours do - each hit with a weapon raises that weapon's skill, each block their Blocking, each second spent running or swimming their Run or Swim, each jump their Jump, each piece of food put on or taken off a cooking station their Cooking, and each batch of arrows crafted or piece of gear repaired their Crafting, at the same rate as yours, sped up by the world's skill gain setting and by being rested
- Run and Jump work like yours too - up to 25% faster running and 40% stronger jumps at full skill - and their gear speeds them up or slows them down the way yours does, from a Fenris piece's bonus to iron armor's, a heavy weapon's or a tower shield's penalty, counting half again as much when they run, and nothing for a weapon they've put away - and Cooking and Crafting give the bonus yours do, a chance of one more cooked piece or arrow, up to a quarter of the time at full skill
- Swim eases their stamina in the water the way it eases yours, and makes them swim up to 25% faster at full skill too. In the water they tire the way you do - they have your base stamina plus what their food gives, use it only to swim, get it back once they're out of the water, and drown the way you would once it's gone
- The higher a skill, the harder they hit and the better they block, the same as you
- When one of their skills reaches a new level, they glow and chime around their head like you do, which every player nearby sees and hears - the chime at half your own level-up's volume, coming from them and fading with distance, so you don't take it for yours - and say how proud they are, at most every 30 seconds, in a line nothing else they say covers up while it shows - always within 10 meters of a player, and less often the farther they are from the nearest one, down to never at 40 meters, where nobody would see it
- A Viking who comes back after dying starts over, while one who rises as a zombie with Zombie Apocalypse keeps their skills

### Gear

- They're armed for their biome and always wear armor (the set's chest and legs) and wield a weapon - the set's helmet and cape (50% each) and a shield (40%, unless their weapon takes both hands) are left to chance, but for the helmet and cape from the Mountains on, and everything they have is fully repaired. From the Mountains on, every Viking wears a cape - the set's, or else the biome's own (a wolf fur cape in the Mountains, a lox cape in the Plains, a feather cape in the Mistlands, an Asksvin cloak in the Ashlands and a moose hide cape in the Deep North). Every cape of the Mountains and the Deep North resists frost, so their Vikings don't freeze there
- A Viking with a bow or crossbow carries 100 arrows or bolts of their biome, and never carries weapons or ammo that deal spirit damage, or Dyrnwyn in the Deep North. They carry no coins, so a living Viking who dies for good drops none
- Their armor protects them as well as yours does you and wears down the same way, taking the edge off poison, fire and spirit damage along with the rest of a hit, and their weapons and shield wear down as they use them
- Poison works on them the way it works on you, wearing off as slowly, so a blob's or leech's poison hurts them no more each second than it hurts you
- Every village starts with a Viking who carries a bow and arrows from their biome, and when no Viking in the village can shoot, the next to come back brings a bow too. What else they start with depends on the biome:
  - In the Meadows, the Viking with the bow also carries a melee weapon, and the rest carry one weapon of any kind
  - In the Black Forest, a third of the Vikings start with only a bow and arrows, a third with only a melee weapon and a third with both. The Viking with the bow starts with only the bow or with both, half the time each
  - In the Swamp, a fifth of the Vikings start with only a bow and arrows, a fifth with only a melee weapon and the rest with both. The Viking with the bow starts with only the bow a quarter of the time, and with both otherwise
  - From the Mountains on, every Viking starts with both - a melee weapon and a bow or crossbow with its arrows or bolts, taken from the nearest biome below when theirs has none, like a Plains Viking's Draugr Fang
- Each living Viking starts with a torch of their own, which burns down while they hold it, the same as yours - about a night's worth - and is gone once it burns out (see [Torches and Circlets](#torches-and-circlets))
- Some Vikings start with a Dverger circlet in place of a torch, more of them the harder their biome: none in the Meadows, 10% in the Black Forest, 30% in the Swamp, 50% in the Mountains, 70% in the Plains and every one from the Mistlands on
- A Viking of the Mistlands starts with a wisp light worn on their belt, which floats beside them and pushes back the mist the way yours does
- Some Vikings start with a strength belt (Megingjord), more of them the harder their biome: none in the Meadows, 20% in the Black Forest, 40% in the Swamp, 60% in the Mountains, 80% in the Plains and every one from the Mistlands on. It goes in the same slot as a wisp light, so a Mistlands Viking keeps theirs in their pack and puts it on for a quest outside the Mistlands, carrying home 150 more
- Some start with Haldor's wider pockets, a 5th row in their inventory - 20% in the Mountains, 40% in the Plains, 60% in the Mistlands, 80% in the Ashlands and every one in the Deep North - and some with his deeper pockets as well, a 6th row - 20% in the Mistlands, 40% in the Ashlands and 60% in the Deep North. Deeper pockets only come with wider ones. A village buys any of these from Haldor for a Viking without them (see [Quests](#quests))
- What a Viking wears does for them what it does for you - a strength belt (Megingjord) raises how much they carry, gear that raises a skill raises theirs, gear that adds armor or speeds healing does the same for them, and gear that changes speed, resistances, stagger, fall damage, jumping, noise or attack damage works as it does on any creature. With nothing in their utility slot, they put on a belt or other utility item that does something when worn, from loot they pick up or a chest, the way they take up better armor - each keeps to the one they wear, so a spare goes in a chest for another Viking
- What they carry - wood and loot - is in their inventory. A Viking who rises as a zombie keeps it, along with their armor, weapon and shield, and drops the wood and loot when their zombie dies, while a Viking who stays dead drops them where they fall

### Names

- Every new living Viking comes with a Norse name of their own for a woman or a man - Astrid, Dagny, Sigrid or Brünnhilde, Åke, Einar, Ragnar or Ulfric, among about 370 (see [NAMES.md](NAMES.md)) - one no other Viking of their village goes by, and none they'd turn down below. The first four Vikings of a village named Arendelle are Elsa, Anna, Kristoff and Olaf, in that order, each a man or a woman to suit their name. A new Elsa wears the Long Braid, with the blondest hair a Viking is given, and a new Anna wears Twin Braids, with her hair red. Elsa, whatever her byname, is immune to frost - frost damage and the frost effect of a hit pass her by, and she never gets Cold or Freezing, so freezing weather never sends her to the fire or has her looking for gear that resists frost
- Each village has one leader, with a strong leader's byname after their name - Bloodaxe, Ironside, Skull-Splitter, Stormcloak, Draugr-Bane, the Wise, the Strong or the Bold - like Rolf Bloodaxe. The other new Vikings sometimes have one of the other bynames below after their name anyway, keeping it wherever they go - 20% of those in the Meadows, 10% more for each harder biome (30% in the Black Forest, 40% in the Swamp and so on), up to 90% in the Deep North. The first Viking named once their village is known becomes its leader, and when the leader dies, the next Viking born there or joining takes it up; a Viking with a leader's byname who joins a village that already has a leader goes back to their own name
- A Viking whose name another Viking goes by as they join a village, coming back or moving in, gets one of the other bynames after it instead - the Red, the Tall, the Lucky, the Far-Travelled, the Quiet, the Stout, the Old, the Young or Greycloak, or Silkbeard for a bearded Viking - so Rolf becomes Rolf the Red, or a new name when every byname that fits is taken too. A name you gave them is kept as it is, and a name you give with a leader's byname makes that Viking the leader
- Rename a living Viking with the same keys as renaming a tamed creature (Shift + E) - up to 24 characters, room for a byname like "Alfric Stormbreaker", and any "$" in the name is left out. While the box to name them is open, they stop and stand facing you, unless a fight calls them away - and the box closes if they get more than 8 meters from you. They turn down being named Viking or having their name cleared, and keep the name they have
- They won't take the name of a creature (a Troll, or a Zombie anything, though Bear and Bjørn are old names they're happy with), of a god, giant or beast of the myths, a boss or someone you meet like Odin, Thor, Freyr, Fenrir, Eikthyr, Moder, Haldor or Hildir, of a legendary weapon like Dyrnwyn, of a biome like the Meadows or the Black Forest (in English or your game's language), of a place from the lore - Valhalla, Asgard, Midgard, Helheim, Jotunheim, Niflheim, Muspelheim, Alfheim, Vanaheim, Svartalfheim, Bifrost, Yggdrasil or Valheim - of any player who has been in the world, or of another Viking of their village - they turn the name down with a joke and keep the one they had. A name with a text tag in it, like `<color=red>`, they turn down too - "{name} doesn't sound like a name", with the tag shown as you typed it. They turn a name down even in their sleep, so you always hear why it didn't take
- A Viking who doesn't know you yet shows as Viking (?) - over their health bar, when you look at them, on their inventory and on other mods' markers - until they greet you, you talk to them or you name them, and only then does their name show. Opening their inventory or the box to name them has them greet you first, unless they're asleep or in a fight - and one that can't greet you still gets to know you as you name them, so you see the name you give them
- Their name shows over their health bar and when you look at them, they answer to it, they introduce themselves by it to strangers, and the other Vikings call them by name when they talk to them - and talk about them to each other

### Meeting and Talking

- When you come into a village after being away from its Vikings for 3 minutes, the first Viking within 10 meters who sees you turns, waves and greets you - just the one, so the whole village doesn't. One asleep, after an enemy or swimming doesn't - and one who was swimming when you came doesn't greet you once they're out of the water either. Each Viking remembers the players they've greeted: one who has never met you greets you as a stranger, and one who has greets you by name and welcomes you back
- Look at a living Viking and press the use key (E) to talk to them. One who has never met you greets you as a stranger and remembers you, one who knows you answers you by name, one asleep in bed mumbles, and one in a fight or swimming is too busy. Whatever they say, they stop and stand facing you for 4 seconds while you read it. Now and then one who knows you asks how you're doing, or talks about one of the runestones of the biome they're standing in - the boar stone of the Meadows, Ulf's stones and the rest. A Viking who shares a name with someone on the runestones, like Ulf, Astrid, Bjørn, Harald or Gudrun, sometimes talks about the stones of the one with their name. A Viking named Elsa, Anna, Kristoff, Olaf or Hans sometimes says something in place of a runestone that nods to the film Frozen - Anna asks if you wanna build a snowman, Elsa tells you to let it go, and Kristoff talks about the rock trolls who raised him. Elsa also scolds a stone golem she fights as if it were Marshmallow, the snow giant she made - "Marshmallow! No!" A Viking whose village goes by their name, whatever their byname, sometimes tells you how strange it is to hear their name everywhere, or brags that the village is named after them, and talks it over with the others too - who tease them about it in turn. Come by with nothing on your chest or legs and the nearest Viking who sees you asks if you're cold and offers to lend you something to wear, and those you talk to sometimes do too - kindly, never poking fun. With a wooden training weapon in hand, they encourage you the same way - glad to see you keep your skill sharp, and training too
- Each Viking remembers when they met you - when they greeted you or you talked to them for the first time - and how many times you've talked to them, counting talks at least a minute apart. Once they've known you for 30 game days, or you've talked to them more than 10 times, you're old friends, and now and then they greet you or answer you as one
- What they say to you, to each other and about danger - every line, and when they say it - is listed in [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md), along with the news they pass on (see [News](#news))

### Their Inventory

- Look at a living Viking and press I to open their inventory beside yours - yours on the left, under your character card, and theirs on the right, under a card of their own with their name and a Skills button that shows their skills, with no crafting panel. Their armor and weight show in tabs on the left of their inventory, the way yours show on the right of yours, and both of you get a health tab between them - your health and theirs, now and at most, marked with the heart a tamed animal shows as you pet it - and their status effects show under it the way yours show on your HUD - each one's icon, name and time left, including resting, rested, wet, cold and poisoned - with yours shown the same way under your inventory. The window goes back to its usual layout once it closes
- Their inventory has 4 rows like yours, a 5th with wider pockets and a 6th with deeper pockets as well (see [Gear](#gear))
- What they wear - equipped, or sheathed on their back - is marked the way your equipped items are, and their weight shows with the most they can carry, 300, or 450 with a strength belt on. Move things between them by dragging, or ctrl-click to hand something over or take it, and right-click something they wear to have them take it off, or something they don't to have them put it on, whether or not it's better than theirs - armor or a utility item where it goes, arrows as the ones they shoot, and a weapon or shield in their hands the way you'd hold one, which they put on their back once their inventory is closed and there's no danger. Either takes them as long as it takes you, playing your equipping animation, with the piece marked as queued meanwhile - and a one-handed weapon and a shield go on or come off together, the way your R key draws or sheathes both hands at once. A chest's take-all and place-stacks buttons aren't there
- On their own they always switch to the best gear they have: armor for an empty slot or better than what they wear, a better shield or a better weapon of their kind sitting in their inventory unused - like a piece you took off them, gave them, or had them swap for something worse - they put on after 10 seconds, taking the same time, once their inventory is closed. A pickaxe is only ever a tool to them, kept for mining - they never take one up as a weapon or fight with one
- While it's open they stop and stand facing you and leave their gear as it is - they don't put anything on, draw or sheathe their weapon, or pull out a torch - until you close it with I or the game's own keys, or it closes as a chest's does when they're called away to a fight and get too far from you. Whatever you take that they were wearing, they take off

### Hurting a Viking

- Like the dvergr, they fight zombies and monsters and leave you alone - until you hurt one, which turns every Viking and dvergr nearby against you. Unlike the dvergr, a Viking calms down again once they've slept on it - one angered by day forgives you the next morning, and one angered at night as the next night falls, as long as no player has hurt them since
- Like you, they never take a critical hit: a staggered Viking takes no more damage than one on their feet, where the game doubles the damage to any other staggered creature - and a monster that catches them unawares gets no sneak attack bonus on them, the same as on you
- The damage they take is scaled the way yours is: by the world's enemy damage setting and the players nearby, as monsters hit harder for each one - not cut the way a creature's is as more players come, nor by the world's player damage setting
- You can only hurt a living Viking with PvP on (the PvP toggle on your inventory screen), the same as hurting another player, so a stray swing or arrow with PvP off won't turn the village against you. The first time a hit of yours does nothing because PvP is off, Hugin flies in to explain, once per character. A Viking that's already hostile can always be fought back
- The console's kill commands, and the K key in `debugmode`, only kill the living Vikings who are angry at you while your PvP is off, and every living Viking with it on - they say how many they spared, and the first time they spare any, Hugin flies in to explain. This covers kill commands other mods put in place of the game's too, like Server Devcommands'

### Coming Back and Joining

- A Viking who dies comes back, one at a time - a day after the last one came back or after they died, whichever is later, or three days for a Viking a player killed - up to as many as the village started with, so a village that's wiped out fills up again. A Viking only comes back while no hostile creature is in the village or within 10 meters of it (a fallen Viking's own zombie included) - unless one has been there for 10 minutes with no raid on, like the stragglers a raid leaves behind or the village's own dead risen where they fell, when they come back anyway to take it on - at a spot with no hostile creature within 20 meters, and only while a house of the village's core still stands - with at least half its pieces standing. A Viking calls a standing house home, moving to the nearest one when their own is razed, and with none standing they rest by the fire until one is rebuilt
- Vikings coming back to a village that hostile creatures have held for those 10 minutes come back together rather than one at a time, so none is sent in alone against more than they can take. While they wait, the Vikings due pile up, still a day apart, and once there are enough of them that with the Vikings at home they outnumber the hostile creatures in the village and within 10 meters of it - or every Viking waiting is due - they all come at once, side by side from the same spot, and walk in together
- A Viking coming to the village - one coming back, or a new one for a bed - starts 8-16 meters outside the farm's fence, at a spot no player is within 20 meters of or can see, and walks in, so living in the village doesn't keep them away. After 2 minutes with no such spot, they come outside the fence anyway, even near you or where you can see them. Only a village with no open ground outside its fence, like one ringed by water or cliffs, has them start beside a house of its core instead. They never start in or right beside a fire's flames, and never spawn or come back inside a pen or anywhere else fenced in and open to the sky
- A living Viking who belongs to no village, like one you spawn, joins the village they're within 60 meters of, calling the nearest house of its core home - taking the place of one the village is waiting to get back, or else staying on as an extra who isn't brought back when they die - once the village has had every Viking it starts with, so one who comes before that waits outside rather than take a new Viking's place. One on the road to a village joins only that one, passing through any other (see [Vikings With No Village](#vikings-with-no-village))
- Every Viking who joins a village after it's set up - born there, brought by a bed, coming back, or arriving from the road - is new there for a game day, and the others welcome them and talk about them when they talk (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)), naming the village their group came from when it was one you disbanded
- A Viking who's been more than 60 meters from their own village for 5 minutes - led or pushed away, say - joins the village they're in the same way, and their old village gets one back in their place as it would for a death

### Debug Mode

- With `debugmode` on, a living Viking's job shows on a line under their name, in light gray - over their health bar, which moves their name up to make room, when you look at them, and on markers from mods that show their name
- Looking at any Viking shows what they're doing - their health, target, whether they're alerted, aggravated, asleep or in danger, what they hold, and for a living Viking their ID - a GUID of their own, apart from their name, which the news they've told goes by - their village, home, job and how long they've been at it, how long they've been standing still when it's more than 10 seconds, whether they're ready for a raid, the foods they've eaten with how long each has left and how much they heal them, whether they're resting by a fire or rested and for how long, how much faster that makes them heal, the forsaken powers they know, which they're under and when they can use one again, what's moving them and whether they're twitching - stopping and starting or turning back and forth - which is also written to the log with what they were doing, and the loot lying around the village - how much it would take, and why it leaves the rest
- Looking at a village's bonfire shows the village's state - which client runs it, its houses, the Vikings loaded and how many are hurt or hostile, its head count and the Vikings it's waiting to get back and when the next is due, tonight's watch, the fire's fuel and cooking stations, its pieces noted, missing and damaged, what's still to be set up, whether enemies are near, the beds players built for it - how many brought a Viking, and why the rest haven't yet - and the raid going on, if any: where it is, how far past the village's edge, whether it's running or paused with no player near, and whether it's on the village - a path a house added for a player's bed still needs, with who's making it or what it's waiting on, its quest - when it's due back, where its Vikings are on the way there, at work or on the way home, and what each of them takes, works with and brings home - or how long until it looks for one, and every group of Vikings on its way to it, out of the world or walking the road - what brings them, their names, where they are and when they're expected. Its times count down by the second. What would only say there's nothing to report is left out, like tonight's watch by day before one is picked, counts of none, and enemies when there are none near
- The large map (M) pins every group on the road or out on a quest where it is now, labeled with how many, what brings them, their names and what they're doing, moving the pins every 2 seconds
- Press D at a village's bonfire to show the village's state in a sidebar down the left of the screen instead, between your hotbar and your food, on a see-through black like the console's and behind the rest of the HUD and the game's windows - shown whenever you're in a village, for the village you're in, and left out of the bonfire's hover text - until you press D at a village's bonfire again. It shrinks its text to fit, and lasts until you close the game
- Hover text longer than 12 lines is shown twice as wide and centered on the crosshair, so it stays on screen at the same size

## Viking AI

How living Vikings see, fight, get around and look after themselves. What they do with their day - every job, in the order they pick them - is in [JOBS.md](JOBS.md), and the work it adds up to under [Village Work](#village-work). What's different in each biome is under [By Biome](#by-biome).

### Senses

- Vikings see 50 meters. In the Mistlands' mist they see far less (see [Mistlands](#mistlands))
- Asleep they don't see anything, but they wake to an enemy's noise, to being hit, and to anyone nearby being attacked
- A monster lying asleep until someone comes near - like an abomination disguised as roots and logs in the Swamp - wakes for a living Viking coming as close as you would wake it, and goes for them, so the Vikings fight it rather than walk past it

### Picking Fights

- They don't pick fights they don't need: a living Viking only goes after a zombie or monster that's a threat - one in their village, within 10 meters of them, alerted, or after someone - and leaves the rest alone unless they attack the Viking or someone the Viking is called to help
- When a Viking or player within 30 meters is attacked by a zombie or monster, the Vikings rush to help, whatever they're doing - even asleep. They don't chase an enemy more than 40 meters past the village's edge (70 meters for one that hurt them or someone they're defending), or into the water - they stop at the shore, and leave alone an enemy out in water more than a meter deep, like a Neck swimming in a river, though archers still shoot at it from the shore - and walk back when they end up far outside it
- A zombie or monster attacking a building piece in a village - one of the village's own, or one you built there - calls the Vikings within 30 meters of the piece to fight it, the same as when it hurts one of them, at most every 3 seconds for each attacker
- A Viking with no enemy of their own who sees another Viking within 20 meters fighting one joins in, unless they're in bed, resting or badly hurt - though never a hunter's prey, which they leave to the hunter
- A Viking running home from a fight for any reason, hanging back below a quarter of their health, or fighting a monster too tough for them to bring down alone shouts for help every 6 seconds while enemies that fight back are near, never over prey that only runs, like a deer or a hare - "Help!" and the like over their head - bringing every Viking within 30 meters of them to help
- A Viking who goes after an enemy calls it out by name - "Watch out - Neck!" - at most every 15 seconds, and only when no other Viking has called out that enemy in the last minute

### Away From Home

- Out of the village - foraging, hunting, or getting wood or loot - a Viking leaves alone whatever has a monster within 50 meters of it, and heads home as soon as one comes that close or they end up with an enemy to fight, so they're back before most monsters spot them rather than leading a pack home. A single greyling with no other monster within 50 meters doesn't count - a Viking carries on with what they're doing and fights it where they are
- They don't fight a losing fight away from home. Outside the village's core, a Viking runs back to its middle for help when they're attacked with no other Viking within 15 meters, when 4 or more enemies are on them, when they're below a quarter of their health, or when a Viking with them is - which brings their companions home with them. One who ran for being alone turns to fight as soon as another Viking is with them, and the rest once they're home with another Viking of their village within 6 meters - home with no one beside them, a Viking runs on to the nearest of their fellows rather than turning to fight alone at the edge of the village, and only stands alone when none of them is left
- The strongest head out most. A Viking from a harder biome than the village's, by the gear they came with, goes out for wood, food and prey 3 times as often as the rest, since they're the safest out there - see [Rolled Jobs](JOBS.md#rolled-jobs)
- A village with more than 2 Vikings always keeps one at home: a Viking only heads out of the village for wood, food or loot while another is in the village and not heading out themselves. Fights still take them out as far as they need

### Hanging Back

- At home, a Viking below a quarter of their health hangs back and lets the others take the fight, until they're back above 40% - unless there are more enemies than healthy Vikings near, or none of them are healthy, when they rejoin the fight. Hanging back, they don't just watch: they circle around behind an enemy in the village's core that's busy with someone else, never crossing in front of it, and strike it from behind. As soon as an enemy comes after them, they run - keeping about 5 meters from the fight, or 20 meters from an enemy that shoots or throws, like a greydwarf, a greydwarf shaman or a zombie Viking with a bow - and go back to striking from behind 3 seconds after nothing's after them

### Weapons

- While all is quiet they carry their weapon and shield sheathed on their back, and draw them as soon as there's danger
- They raise their shield against attacks, more often and with better timing the more skilled they are. They know the reach of what they fight with, closing in until their swing will land before they strike - right up close with a knife or their fists, farther off with a polearm - and swing as fast as you do, again as soon as their last swing ends, stringing their weapon's combo together
- Vikings have no fixed roles. One with a melee weapon and a bow with arrows picks between them for each fight: the bow against a root, against an enemy in deep water, flying or with no way to it, against one 3 of their fellows are already at, against a tough monster they're kiting, and against anything while they're below half their health, even when the bow hurts less; their melee weapon when the enemy comes within 3 meters, until it's 6 meters off again; and otherwise whichever hurts most, by its damage and their skill with it. They know what each enemy is weak to, resists and is immune to, and weigh every weapon through it - picking the melee weapon and the bow and arrows that hurt that enemy most, like a club over a sword against something that shrugs off slashes, and counting a monster as tough by how hard they can really hit it. An archer, here and in [JOBS.md](JOBS.md), is any Viking who can shoot
- They use their melee weapon's power attack when it's a smart move. A staggered enemy takes double damage, so the power attacks that stagger - an atgeir's spin and a battleaxe's and a mace's - go on an enemy with a fellow Viking or a player within 4 meters of it to make the most of the stagger, and an atgeir spins whenever it would catch 2 or more enemies, alone or not. Alone against a single enemy they don't stagger it, since an atgeir's spin knocks it back out of reach before they can follow it up. The power attacks that hit hard - a sword's and a knife's lunge, an axe's and a mace's overhead blow - go on an enemy that's staggering, and otherwise whenever they're ready, unless someone at the enemy holds a weapon that staggers, when the Viking saves the hard hit for the stagger. That makes a Viking with an atgeir the one who keeps the enemy staggered while the others cut it down. None of it is wasted: no stagger on an enemy that's already staggering or can't be staggered, never against something flying, and a spear is never thrown, which would leave it on the ground. They spend no stamina, so after each power attack they wait as long as you'd take to get its stamina back, at least 3 seconds - about 5 seconds for an atgeir's spin, 4 for a sword's lunge
- An archer slightly prefers shooting back at enemy archers - draugr and skeleton archers, or zombie Vikings with bows - over a nearer enemy, counting one as a fifth nearer than it is when picking who to go after
- With Zombie Apocalypse and more than one kind of arrow, a Viking readies the ones that hurt the enemy in front of them most (see [With Zombie Apocalypse](#with-zombie-apocalypse))

### Tough Monsters and Roots

- A Viking weighs up each monster they fight. It's tough when it would take more of their swings to bring down than they could take of its hardest hits - each side's hits through the other's armor, the monster's resistances counted, and a Viking's swing with the weapon they'd fight with, a bow counted for less since it shoots more slowly, and their fists only when they have no weapon, while a monster's bow is counted with the best arrows it carries and a zombie Viking's hits as hard as its skill and biome make them - so a troll, bear or brute is tough for a Viking in the Meadows' rags and flint, and not for one in the Plains' padded armor and black metal, and an armored zombie Viking that shrugs off spears is tough for the Mountains' Vikings
- A tough monster is fought as a group when the village has the Vikings for it: they work out how many of them it takes to bring it down before it brings the first of them down, and half as many again - counting the health it has left, so fewer are needed as it weakens. While that many are within 30 meters of a Viking - in reach of their shouts for help - but not yet gathered against it, those there keep clear and call the rest in, and once they're all within 26 meters past its reach they charge it together
- With too few for that, when someone near can shoot, they kite it: the Viking it's after lures it, backing off at a run whenever it comes within 1.5 meters of its reach - a troll's reach is long - and holding once it's 4 meters past, turning to the side when cornered. The others keep 8 meters past its reach and farther from it than the lure, so it stays after the lure, and shoot it. One with nothing to shoot with holds their distance rather than walk in
- A Viking with nothing that resists poison - no root armor or poison resistance mead - kites a blob or oozer the same way whenever someone near can shoot it, however weak it is, rather than stand in its poison splash. One who resists poison fights it hand to hand as usual, and with no one to shoot it, so do the rest
- A Viking fighting hand to hand never goes for a root, or any other monster that can't move - they keep 6 meters from it, out of its reach, stepping back out if they find themselves closer, and when one is within 15 meters they shout for an archer, and the village's nearest archer comes to shoot it. Archers keep out of its reach too - one within 6 meters of a root backs off, still aiming at it, and shoots it from there. With no archer in the village, they warn the others to keep clear of it

### Hazards

- They keep out of fires' flames. Their paths bend around a lit fire to keep a meter clear of its flames, rather than clipping a bonfire's as they pass, while other creatures find their way as usual. Vikings cook over a fire, make meals at the cauldron over it and add fuel from outside its flames, and a Viking who gets within half a meter of flames anyway - shoved by a crowd, say - runs straight out, even in a fight, until they're a meter clear
- A Viking on fire runs for water within 24 meters to put themselves out, even in the middle of a fight
- A fall hurts them like it hurts you: nothing up to 4 meters, then more the farther they fall, up to 100 damage from 20 meters, through their armor and less with a feather cape. So they watch their step: a Viking about to step off a drop of more than 2 meters turns aside, or stops when every way is a drop. A drop into water deep enough to swim in is fine, unless it's the Ashlands' boiling sea, the weather is freezing and their gear doesn't resist frost, or they couldn't swim back out to where they were standing. Dvergr Vikings don't take fall damage, like the Dvergr they live with
- They keep off the Ashlands' lava and out of its boiling sea (see [Ashlands](#ashlands))
- With Zombie Apocalypse, they keep clear of poison clouds and of the corpses that might leave one (see [The Dead](#the-dead))

### Getting Around

- They walk while all is quiet at home, and run when there's danger - when they've spotted an enemy or been hurt in the last 10 seconds - and whenever they're out of the village's core, so they're out in the open for as little time as they can be. In the rain they hurry everywhere out from under a roof, back to a walk once they're under one. They also run up any slope of 20 degrees or more, so they make it up
- They open the doors in their way and close them behind them, except a door a player built inside an active ward, which they leave shut, and go through a doorway one at a time - one who finds another Viking going through, or someone standing in the doorway, waits beside the door until it's clear, for up to 8 seconds - the next goes as soon as the one crossing is through, and another Viking waiting to go through doesn't count, so two never wait for each other. A Viking stuck for a few seconds beside a door shuts it when it stands open across their way, or opens it when it's shut in front of them, once each time they get stuck - never one someone else is standing in or another Viking is going through, one their own way goes through or one they're walking into - and when that doesn't free them, they go on to jump what's in their way or give up as usual
- They keep off sloped roofs - they never walk over one out of a fight, even when they've spotted an enemy or been hurt but have no enemy to fight, and step off one they find themselves on, climbing down when the ground is right below them or they can't walk to it, as on a roof that comes down to the ground - though in a fight they'll cross one when it's the way to their enemy
- They don't stand about in water either - one who stays put in it for a few seconds with no danger near, talking or standing about, stops and walks to the nearest dry ground, while one wading across a stream on their way somewhere keeps going
- When there's no way around something low in their way, like a pen's fence, they back up, take a run at it and jump it with their own jump - backing up only where the ground behind them doesn't fall away, and jumping from where they stand above a slope they'd slide down - judging where to jump from their speed, their Jump skill and how high it is, so even a Viking with no Jump skill clears a wood fence. They never jump onto a roof, a beam, a chest, a bed, a crafting station or other furniture, which would leave them stuck on top, and one who ends up stuck on top of something anyway, turning back and forth at the edge right above the way they're going, climbs down to it
- A Viking in their village who has tried to walk somewhere for a minute and a half without getting anywhere - shut in beside their bed by the furniture, or at the far side of a pen - gets out to the nearest spot with a way to the village's open ground. When the spot they're stuck on has a way there already, whatever holds them is something the spots around them share, so they're put on the village's open ground itself
- They walk around the creatures and players in their way rather than pushing into them - except the enemy they're fighting - and a Viking held up by others standing about, strolling, talking, petting a boar or standing at the fire, or by one of them in the doorway they're waiting at, asks them to step aside, which they do
- They don't stand about inside a house by day, where they'd be in the way of its chests and door - they stroll out instead, except in the rain (see [Weather](#weather))
- They keep out of pens and anywhere else fenced in and open to the sky, except to forage, pet the boars, slaughter livestock and pick up loot or wood - a Viking foraging, petting a boar, slaughtering one or picking up loot or wood jumps the fence into a pen to get there - loot lying on top of a pen's fence included, and loot open to the sky in the village with no way to it from the village's open ground, like the far side of a big pen - and so does one fighting with a melee weapon whose enemy is on the other side of a pen's fence - they go in by the gate when there's one, or else jump the fence, rather than swinging at the enemy through it, and fight as usual if they can't get over within 10 seconds. One walking into a pen with no way in goes to a side of its fence low enough to jump from, with a way there, rather than the nearest spot outside it, which on a hillside can be at the foot of a bank below the fence - trying the next side when they can't get to one or jump from it, and walking at it as usual once there's none left. One slaughtering livestock always gets into its pen before striking it, whatever they fight with, and never strikes it through the fence - when they can't get over within 30 seconds, they leave that animal alone for a while. The rest never jump a fence into one, and only stroll to spots they can walk back from. One who finds themselves in a pen with nothing to do there stops what they're doing and walks or jumps out. A Viking shut in a pen - with no way out to the village's open ground but over the fence - who has jumped at the fence 3 times without getting out climbs over it on their next try

### Torches and Circlets

- When it's dark they pull out a torch while they aren't fighting, beside a one-handed weapon or in place of another - from just before sunset until dawn, while they're away from their village's fire and its gathering fires and not in bed - and put it away by day. In a fight they put it away for a shield or a two-handed weapon, but keep it beside a one-handed weapon with nothing else in their off hand - and one that starts a fight without a torch out, like a Viking woken by the night watch, pulls one out there
- Once their only torch is half burnt, or they have none, they craft another from its recipe, with wood and resin from the village's chests, using up the more burnt one first. When the village has no resin, they go hunting greylings for it, one Viking at a time. At dusk and at night they craft one too, from what they carry and the chests hold rather than going out for it in the dark: one with no torch at all before going to bed, and the night watch, before keeping watch, whenever their torches don't have enough burn left between them to last the rest of their shift - including partway through it, when they stop patrolling to craft one. A Viking with no torch and nothing to make one with goes without, trying again a minute later
- A Viking with a Dverger circlet wears it in place of their helmet whenever they'd pull out a torch, and puts their helmet back on by day, in bed and as soon as there's danger. A Viking with a circlet never crafts a torch, and one without takes up a circlet they find as loot or in the village's chests

### Rest

- They rest and get rested like you do - resting while they sit by a fire, or stand sheltered by one, and rested after a little while of it or after a night in a bed, for as long as the comfort around them gives you. Both boost their healing as much as they boost yours
- A Viking below 75% health goes to sit by the village's fire, or one of its gathering fires, before anything else by day, in its warmth, until they're rested, and at dusk they sit down there straight away
- Now and then a Viking whose rested bonus has run out goes to sit by the fire to get it back, unless it's late in the day

### Weather

- The weather gets to them like it gets to you: they get wet in the rain out from under a roof, and cold, or freezing in freezing weather, away from a fire's warmth or out of shelter, unless their armor resists frost or they're somewhere warm and cozy. These are the game's own effects, doing to them what they do to you - being wet, cold or freezing slows their healing as much as it slows yours - and keep them from resting, unless somewhere warm and cozy lets them rest while wet. A fire dries them off faster, the same as you
- They keep from freezing: in freezing weather, unless their gear resists frost, they leave their work by day and sit in the warmth of the village's fire, or one of its gathering fires, until the weather clears - or stay inside their house when no fire is burning, or when they couldn't get into its warmth within 10 seconds, trying the fire again after a minute. One who starts freezing at work drops it to warm up, unless they're eating or lightening their load. At dusk and at night they gather by the fire and sleep in their beds as usual, and the night watch keeps watch from a spot in the warmth of the fire, or inside their house when no fire is burning, rather than patrolling - still listening for you, looking into noises, raising the alarm and changing at midnight, and going back there after each
- They keep out of the rain. Since a wet Viking can't rest, one below 75% health rests under a roof in the rain - by the fire when their place there is under one, or else sitting on their house's floor, dry, until they're no longer badly hurt or the rain stops, when they go to the fire to rest. They don't stroll, pet the boars or go to the fire to get rested again in the rain, and stand about inside their house instead, at least 2 meters from its doors so they're out of the doorway, the way they pick a spot on its floor to rest on at night
- They talk indoors too: a Viking looking for someone to talk to in the rain goes to one standing under a roof when there is one, or else takes the other inside their own house to talk, clear of its doors - and with no house to talk in, doesn't start a talk out in the rain
- Foraging, hunting, stocking the chests and the rest of their work go on in the rain, but in a thunderstorm they leave making paths and building beehives for later, and at dusk gather inside their houses rather than out by the fire - in light rain they still gather by the fire. The night watch keeps patrolling in the rain, a thunderstorm included
- They talk about the weather too, now and then greet you or answer you with a word about it, and mutter when they get wet, start freezing or warm up again (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md))

### Eating

- They eat like you do: a Viking below 75% health first goes to a chest with food in it and eats the food that heals fastest, eating each again once half of it has worn off. While the village's chests hold at least three foods for every Viking, the others keep their food up too - otherwise it's left for the wounded - eating a food once a place for one is free or one of theirs has nearly run out, with a tenth of it left, so the village's food lasts. Keeping their food up, they eat the weakest food in the chests that still raises them above their least max health below, so the strong food is left for the wounded and for fights - and with none that does, the strongest of the rest - but only food that does them good: food that raises their max health, or any while they're below it, to heal. At full health they leave food too weak to lift them past their least max health in the chests. They eat up to three different foods at once, each lasting as long as it lasts you - the night skipped when you sleep doesn't count, for their food or their rest, just as it doesn't for yours, and neither does the time a Viking spends out of every player's reach, when they do nothing, so you come back to a village fed as you left it. Their max health works like yours - a base of 25 with each food's health on top, fading as it wears off - and their food heals them as it heals you, every 10 seconds, boosted by resting and being rested, so a Viking who hasn't eaten doesn't heal. A village can't make every food you can, so a Viking's max health never drops below what the food of the biome before theirs would give them:

  | Biome | Least max health | Fully fed on their own biome's best 3 foods |
  |---|---|---|
  | Meadows | 50 | 115 |
  | Black Forest | 115 | 155 |
  | Swamp | 155 | 175 |
  | Mountains | 175 | 195 |
  | Plains | 195 | 235 |
  | Mistlands | 235 | 270 |
  | Ashlands | 270 | 325 |
  | Deep North | 325 | 350 |

  Food better than their own biome's - something you leave in their chests, say - raises them further, for as long as it lasts

### Dusk and Night

- As the sun goes down they gather around the fire facing it, and a few sit down once the torches are out - on a free sitting log, bench or chair by the fire when there is one, or else on the ground, turning to whoever sits beside them. A Viking resting by the fire by day takes a seat the same way. They leave a seat a player sits on. Pushed out of place, they walk back to it
- Each Viking gathers at the gathering fire nearest their home that has room for them - about 17 around a bonfire and 10 around a campfire - or else the one with the most room. When more gather at a fire than it has room for, they spread out in a wider ring around it
- At night each lies down in a free bed in the village's core - one no player has claimed - or else sits on a chair or the floor inside their own house, getting back up and returning to their spot if pushed off it. Each Viking gets a bed of their own as soon as the village has loaded around you - a free one in their own house first, then the nearest - which they go back to every night and the others leave alone, and it shows as "Viking Bed", or "Ella's Bed" for a Viking named Ella once you've met them, in place of "Unclaimed Bed". You can still claim it, even with them lying in it - they get straight up and find another. A bed that isn't sheltered enough to claim shows "(not sheltered)" after its name, and your own bed has an Unclaim option on the alternate use key (Shift+E), which also takes your spawn point back to the start when it was there
- They don't all go to bed or get up at once - each heads to bed up to a minute after night falls, staying by the fire until then, and gets up up to a minute after dawn, most of them early. When you sleep through the night, those who hadn't gone to bed yet stay up: in the morning you wake to, no one else heads to bed, and anyone still on their way turns back. In the morning they get up one by one, a few seconds apart, stepping off their bed onto the floor where they stood to lie down, and half of them stretch
- Each night one Viking for every 10 in the village keeps watch - one for up to 10 Vikings, two for 11-20, and so on - the strongest first: a Viking come from a harder biome than the others, like a Swamp Viking who moved into a Meadows village, keeps watch before them, and the one who takes over at midnight is the strongest of those left, at random among equals. Another is picked in the place of one who dies before night falls - once it's night, no one is woken in their place, and one who dies on their way to wake the next leaves them asleep. A Viking who's awake and sees a watcher die, within 30 meters with nothing in the way, takes their place with a word, as long as they haven't kept watch that night - the nearest one, when several see it
- At midnight the watch changes: each one keeping watch walks over to a Viking who hasn't kept watch that night, wakes them with a word, waits while they get up, and goes to bed, so no one keeps watch twice in a night. When they can't get to them, or haven't in 90 seconds, the watch changes anyway. One with no one left to take over keeps watch until dawn
- The watch patrols from door to door around the village's core and past the fire with a torch out, each starting from the stop nearest them. A watcher who hears you - within the range of the noise you're making, sneaking or not, and not already in plain sight within 10 meters - calls out, walks over to where they heard you, and once they see you within 10 meters says who you are, or that you're a traveler if they've never met you, or that there's nothing there when they get there and find no one, then looks around a moment and goes back to their patrol. They come over for your noise at most once every 3 minutes, so they aren't always coming to check on you. When one goes after a threat near the village, they roar a war cry naming the enemy - "To arms! Greydwarf!" - and wake every Viking in the village

### Raids

- They know when a raid is on. When one of the game's raids - the zombie Viking raid among them - reaches over the village, every Viking who's awake draws their weapon and drops whatever they're doing but what can't wait - keeping watch, eating or resting by the fire while hurt, and lightening their load - to keep guard: they form up around the fire they gather at, facing out and looking around - Vikings who can shoot in the ring they gather in at dusk, and those fighting hand to hand 3 meters farther out, in front of them - each on their own side of the fire, shifting a little every 20-40 seconds, and in a village with no fire to gather at, at a spot in its core, and take up no other work, go out for nothing and mourn no one until the raid is over
- They fight a raid together. While one is on, a Viking keeping guard goes to any fight one of the village's Vikings is in, and answers any call for help, anywhere in the village or within 20 meters past its edge - seen or not, however far off - rather than only those within 30 meters, so no one is left to take on the raid alone at the edge of the village
- By day the first to notice calls it out. At night the watch roars the alarm the moment the raid starts, waking the whole village to keep guard with them rather than going back to bed - a Viking asleep doesn't know of the raid until something wakes them

### The Dead

- They mourn their dead. When a Viking dies and their body stays down, the Vikings of their village within 25 meters - up to 5 of them - stop what they're doing once no enemy is within 20 meters of the body, unless they're keeping watch, asleep, out hunting, cooking or lightening their load. They walk over and stand around the body for 15-25 seconds, kneeling, crying, despairing or bowing their heads, and one of them says goodbye - by name, for a Viking you named. Once they've mourned, they pick up the gear the dead Viking dropped within 8 meters of the body, the way they pick up what an enemy they killed dropped, and put it away in the village's chests. A body only lies for a minute, so after a longer fight there may be no one left to mourn
- They remember their dead. A village keeps the names of its named Vikings for 7 days after they die, and now and then a talk between two of its Vikings is about one of them, remembered fondly - at most once every 15 minutes in a village. A name another Viking of the village has taken since is left out
- With Zombie Apocalypse, they know the dead get back up. With nothing to fight, a Viking who's awake and within 25 meters of a corpse about to rise in or near the village stops what they're doing, stands a few meters off it with their weapon drawn, and goes for the zombie the moment it stands. In a fight, they don't waste blows on a zombie that's still getting up and can't be hurt - a Viking fights another enemy within 15 meters they can hurt, or else stands ready just out of reach (an archer where they stand) and strikes once the zombie is up. When the body of one of their own is about to rise, the first Viking to stand ready by it says goodbye before they fight what gets up
- With Zombie Apocalypse, they keep 3 meters from poison clouds and from zombies' corpses that might leave one as they rot away (from the Black Forest on), stepping away even in a fight and going back to what they were doing once they're clear. Their work doesn't take them any nearer: they wait for the poison to go a little way off, and after 10 seconds give up on the spot and get on with something else

### Forsaken Powers

- When you use a forsaken power, the living Vikings, Dvergr Vikings and Dvergr within 10 meters of you who aren't angry at you get it too, the same as other players near you do, and it does for them what it does for any creature. One of them who's awake thanks you for it, or one or two when there are more. Each Viking who gets it learns it, if they didn't know it already
- A power that reaches you from another player or a Viking says who activated it - "Astrid activates the power of the Elder" - in place of the power's own message, which for Eikthyr's would tell you that you activated it. A Viking who doesn't know you yet is "A Viking"
- They have forsaken powers of their own. A Viking knows the power of their own biome's boss and of every biome's before it - Eikthyr's in the Meadows, up to the Elder's in the Black Forest, up to Yagluth's in the Plains, and all of them from the Ashlands on, since the Deep North has no boss - and learns any other power they get, from you or from another Viking. They can only use one they know, and use it the way you do: it reaches you and the other players they aren't angry at, and the Dvergr, Dvergr Vikings and living Vikings within 10 meters who aren't angry at players - and afterwards they can't use any power until the power's own cooldown is over, 20 minutes for the game's, the night you sleep through not counting, as it doesn't for yours. They say which power they're using as they use it. When more than one would do, they take the first of these they know:
  - In a fight, never against a single enemy or only the Meadows' creatures - greylings, boars, necks, deer and Meadows zombie Vikings - counting the enemies within 20 meters: the Queen's against enemies whose attacks poison, Fader's against fire, Moder's against frost, then Yagluth's, Bonemass's or the Elder's against 3 or more enemies, and the Elder's when they're below half their health
  - As they start foraging, which raises their Farming skill, Yagluth's
  - As their job first takes them out of the village - foraging, hunting, getting wood or loot, cutting trees and the like - Eikthyr's
  - Below half their health with nothing to fight, the Elder's
  - Sitting on their boat as it sails into the wind, with nobody on board under it, Moder's, so the wind blows from behind them - as the boat sets off, and again when it wears off, by whichever of the crew who know it is ready first (see [Sailing](#sailing))
- Only one Viking uses a power at a time - one who would use a power another Viking within 40 meters is in the middle of using waits until they're done, and decides again. A Viking already under a power only uses it when at least 2 other Vikings within 10 meters aren't under it
- At the gathering at dusk, a Viking who knows a power another of their village doesn't, and can use a power, walks over to them and uses it beside them, so they learn it along with everyone else it reaches. Those who learn it go on to teach it to others who don't know it, the same way, and no two Vikings set out to teach the same Viking. Teaching a power is worth using it even when the teacher is under it already

### By Biome

A Viking is of their village's biome (see [Farms](#farms)), which sets their health, skills, gear and food, and the biome they stand in changes how they get by.

#### Meadows

- Skills of 1-12, at least 50 health, and Eikthyr's power, which they use as their job takes them out of the village
- No strength belts or pockets
- The Viking with the bow also carries a melee weapon, and the rest carry one weapon of any kind
- No Dverger circlets - every Viking lights their way with torches
- They hunt boar and necks, and an archer deer too - never greylings, the Black Forest's prey, and never out onto the ground of a harder biome (see [Hunting](#hunting))
- A Meadows village never goes mining, and its workbench is level 2

#### Black Forest

- They know the Elder's power as well as Eikthyr's
- A third of the Vikings start with only a bow and arrows, a third with only a melee weapon and a third with both
- 10% start with a Dverger circlet and 20% with a strength belt, and the village can go on a quest to buy each from Haldor once a week
- Greylings are prey, and a single greyling never sends a Viking out of the village home - they fight it where they are
- The village keeps 4 pickaxes and an axe that cuts fine wood in storage, and gets a cauldron by its fire. A farm in or within 150 meters of the Black Forest or a harder biome is built bigger

#### Swamp

- A fifth of the Vikings start with only a bow and arrows, a fifth with only a melee weapon and the rest with both
- 30% start with a Dverger circlet and 40% with a strength belt
- A leech is never a threat until it hurts you or a Viking, however near it lies, so they don't wade into the Swamp's water after one. Only an archer hunts leeches, shooting from the bank, and other leeches nearby don't put them off. A plain draugr is prey - never an elite or an archer - and its entrails go into sausages
- The Swamp's own rain, which hardly ever lets up, doesn't keep them in: in it they stroll, pet the boars, talk outside and walk rather than hurry, the same as on a dry day - though they still rest under a roof when hurt and don't go out to the fire to get rested again, since they'd only get wet, and a storm in the Swamp keeps them in as it does anywhere
- The village's ground is raised out of the swamp water it stands in (see [Ground, Paths and Bridges](#ground-paths-and-bridges)), and its cooking stations are iron

#### Mountains

- Every Viking starts with both a melee weapon and a bow or crossbow, always wears their set's helmet and a cape, and every cape of the Mountains resists frost
- In freezing weather, a Viking whose gear doesn't resist frost leaves their work to warm up by the fire (see [Weather](#weather)) - and the night watch keeps watch from the fire's warmth rather than patrolling. In freezing weather, a drop into deep water is only safe when their gear resists frost
- Keeping the frost off comes first when they take up gear: while nothing else they wear resists frost, they take up armor that does whatever its armor, and never swap a piece that does for one that doesn't
- 50% start with a Dverger circlet, 60% with a strength belt and 20% with wider pockets, and the village can go on a quest to buy wider pockets from Haldor once a week
- Wolves are prey, and drakes for an archer, but a wolf only with no other monster within 50 meters of it, its own pack included

#### Plains

- 70% start with a Dverger circlet, 80% with a strength belt and 40% with wider pockets
- Prey that isn't only for archers - lox, wolves, moose and the like - is hunted as a party of 3-4, never alone (see [Hunting](#hunting)), and a lox only with no other monster within 50 meters of it, since angering one of a herd could have the whole herd on them. Hunting quests send 3-5
- A Viking a deathsquito comes after meets its dive. One with a shield takes up a one-handed weapon beside it, raises the shield as the deathsquito comes within 8 meters and holds it, then strikes back as soon as the sting is spent. One with a two-handed weapon stands their ground as it dives and times their swing to land just after it comes within reach, from how fast it's coming and how long that weapon's swing takes to land, which they learn from their own swings. One with neither fights it the usual way - an archer shoots it
- The village's ground is raised out of any water it stands in, and dry ground by the sea is never lowered into it

#### Mistlands

- In the Mistlands' mist, a Viking sees only 10 meters, as the Dvergr do - unless they or what they're looking at are within reach of a wisp light, a wisp torch or one of the Dvergr's demisters - though they still hear you coming as far off as always
- Every Viking starts with a Dverger circlet, and a wisp light worn on their belt, which floats beside them and pushes back the mist the way yours does
- Every Viking starts with a strength belt too, which they keep in their pack since the wisp light takes its slot, and put on for a quest outside the Mistlands. 60% start with wider pockets and 20% with deeper pockets as well, and the village can go on a quest to buy deeper pockets from Haldor once a week
- Their village is lit with wisp torches and a wisp fountain (see [Lights Against the Mist](#lights-against-the-mist)), since its fire doesn't push back the mist
- Hares are prey. The Dvergr hire some Vikings here (see [Dvergr Vikings](#dvergr-vikings))

#### Ashlands

- They keep off the Ashlands' lava and out of the boiling sea around the Ashlands, always - even in a fight, or on fire with nowhere else to put it out. Their paths go around both with a meter to spare, they stop short rather than step onto either, they leave alone an enemy standing over lava, and one knocked in gets straight back out. A bridge, floor or rock over the lava is safe ground to them, as it is to you
- A Viking on fire never runs into the boiling sea to put themselves out, and a drop into it is never safe
- Every Viking wears a strength belt, 80% start with wider pockets and 40% with deeper pockets as well
- On the road they never cross the lava, and never sail the boiling sea in a boat it would burn, which only the drakkar holds up in (see [Vikings With No Village](#vikings-with-no-village))
- Asksvin are prey, and voltures for an archer
- The village's paths go around the lava and the boiling sea, crossing on grausten bridges, its bonfire and portal never stand on them, and it has a portal home to an easier village (see [Portals Home](#portals-home))

#### Deep North

- Skills of 88-100, at least 325 health, and every forsaken power, as in the Ashlands
- Every Viking wears a strength belt and has wider pockets, and 60% have deeper pockets as well
- Every cape of the Deep North resists frost, so freezing weather doesn't drive them to the fire the way it does a Viking without one (see [Mountains](#mountains)), and they never carry Dyrnwyn
- They knock the heavy snow off the village's pieces and clear the deep snow on its ground (see [Clear Snow](JOBS.md#clear-snow))
- Moose and seals are prey, a moose only with no other monster within 50 meters of it
- The village's bridges are grausten, and it has a portal home to an easier village (see [Portals Home](#portals-home))

## Village Work

By day the Vikings keep their village going. [JOBS.md](JOBS.md) has every job in the order a Viking picks them, with the numbers each goes by - this is what the work adds up to.

### Repairing and Rebuilding

- Work comes first by day. When a building piece in or around the village is damaged - its houses, fences, pens and fire, or anything a player built there - the nearest Viking takes out a hammer and repairs it, which takes no wood. A piece they can't get within reach of in a minute and a half is left unrepaired for 5 minutes - a chest left so is still one they eat from, store in and take from
- Whatever they can't get to - a piece, loot, a bush - once two or more of them have failed to reach it three times within 10 minutes, every Viking leaves it alone for half an hour, and for half an hour longer each time they give up on it again, up to 2 hours. A Viking who has failed to reach it three times themselves within 10 minutes leaves it alone the same way, whether or not anyone else tried, so one left on their own stops going back to it. A Viking stranded where they can't walk from - up on a roof, or anywhere else off the ground they walk on - doesn't count toward either, and only leaves it alone themselves, for a while. A chest is never given up on that way, since without it they'd have nowhere to put things or eat from, and nor is a cooking station, since the fire's stations are all they cook at - only the Viking who couldn't get to it leaves it alone, for a minute
- An active ward keeps them from repairing or rebuilding anything inside it, the village's fire included, the same as it keeps out other players
- When a piece of the village's own is destroyed, a Viking gathers as much wood as it takes and rebuilds it, lowest first - all but its wood piles, which stay gone once they're taken down or burned. A house with every piece destroyed is built back last, once the rest of the village is whole. The village's fire is built back with nothing (see [The Fire](#the-fire))
- Since the Vikings build from wood they gather for free, every piece put up in a village rather than generated with it - what they rebuild, the furniture, blueprint houses, bridges, cooking stations, sitting logs and beehives - drops nothing when it breaks or is taken down, while the pieces the farm was generated with, and a village's portal and the sign on it, drop as usual until they're rebuilt
- Then they keep the bonfire or campfire stacked, one Viking at a time adding a piece of wood at a time until it's full

### Wood

- For rebuilding and the fire they take wood stored in the village's chests first. Otherwise they gather it from the branches around the village and from wood, fine wood and core wood lying on the ground, carrying up to 20. Picked branches grow back as usual. It's always plain wood they spend - fine wood and core wood they put away as loot, for the recipes that ask for them (see [Wood](JOBS.md#wood)). When there's no wood to be found, they get on with the rest of their day for a while. Wood, loot or food they can't get within reach of - tucked against a rock, say - or that they get stuck on the way to, they leave alone for a minute and go for another
- When the village is out of wood - none carried, none in the chests and none lying about - and a Viking is down to fewer than 20 arrows or the workbench or forge needs building back, one Viking at a time goes lumberjacking: they cut down up to 4 brush trees within 60 meters of the village - small trees that drop their wood when they fall, rather than a log - chopping with an axe the way you would. One with no axe takes one from a chest; with none there, they leave the job for up to 30 seconds to a Viking with an axe who's only standing about, strolling, talking or petting a boar, who stops to take it on; and with nobody to take it, they craft a stone axe, punching the trees for its wood when there's none - trying again after each tree falls. The wood lies where the trees fell for the work that needs it to gather, and an axe they took from a chest or crafted goes back in one
- Now and then, when one of the village's chests has room, a Viking with nothing else to do gathers wood from the ground - up to 20 - and puts it in the chest, one Viking at a time for every 4 in the village. They never fill more than half of a chest's slots with wood, whether stocking it or putting away loot

### Loot and Chests

- Ahead of all their other work but eating, resting when they're hurt and keeping warm in freezing weather - cooking included, though done food is still taken off a cooking station before it burns - they pick up loot lying around the village and put it away in a chest with room for all of it, leaving it where it lies when no chest has room. They use chest space carefully, walking farther to do it: first to the nearest chest whose unfinished stacks of it can take all of it, so no slot is used, then to one with an unfinished stack of it to fill before the rest goes in a slot beside it, then to one that already holds some, keeping it together, and only then to the nearest chest with room. Wood they stock the chests with goes the same way, topping up a stack before starting another, and they take wood from a chest with an unfinished stack first, using up its smallest stack
- When a lot comes in at once - Vikings home from a quest, travelers arriving with what they carried, or a cart emptied - and the village's own chests are left with fewer than 4 free slots between them, a Viking builds another wood chest beside one in a house, up to 4 chests a house (see [Build More Chests](JOBS.md#build-more-chests) in JOBS.md)
- Whenever a Viking opens a chest, they merge its unfinished stacks of each item - two stacks of 30 and 25 wood become 50 and 5 - so the chests don't fill up with half-empty stacks. A chest keeps one slot free for wood until it holds some and one for food until it holds some, so the loot they put away never crowds out what they gather
- They gather everything lying within 15 meters of each other that they can carry before heading to the chests - as much as they can without being slowed by the weight, taking part of a stack when that's all that fits - and a Viking strolling, standing about or petting a boar drops that within 5 seconds of loot turning up. Like a player, a Viking carries no more than 300 weight in all, what they wear included, so wood, loot or food that would take them over is left for another trip or another Viking
- Right after a kill, the Viking who fought the enemy picks up what it drops - anything lying within 8 meters of where it died, for up to 45 seconds after, however far from the village and at any hour, once there's no enemy near - unless they're badly hurt, held back by their load or busy with something they wouldn't drop for loot, and puts it away with the loot near it. The night watch does too, and goes back to its watch after
- For 2 minutes after a fight, a Viking who isn't hurt or keeping watch also drops talking, gathering at the fire, resting for the night, and the work loot comes ahead of - repairing, rebuilding, stacking the fire, stocking the chests, crafting arrows and repairing their gear - to clear up what the fight left, at any hour, and goes back to their work after
- They take what drops from a kill once it has come to rest, at least 2 seconds after it drops, but only take what a player dropped once it has lain there 30 seconds, so what you've just put down is left a while, and never arrows, bolts or fish, and only use public chests that no one has open and you haven't kept them out of (see [Village Chests](#village-chests))
- Gear they pick up that's better than their own, they keep - putting it on takes them as long as it takes you, playing your equipping animation - and right after picking up loot, a Viking goes to a chest holding gear better than their own and takes it up: armor for a slot they have nothing in or with more armor than what they wear there, a shield that blocks better, or a weapon that deals more than their best of the same kind - melee, or a bow or crossbow, counting its arrows. They put what the new gear replaces in a chest instead. In the Mountains or the Deep North, keeping the frost off comes first (see [Mountains](#mountains)). A Viking who dies rises as a zombie with this gear too, and their zombie drops each piece only by its usual gear drop chance
- They only keep so much of what they don't use themselves - anything but wood and food, like resin or hides: at most one stack of it in a chest and two across the village's chests - only one of each trophy - though they'll top up a stack that's already there. Axes and pickaxes are kept up to as many as the village keeps, side by side in a chest. The rest they carry out of the village, past where a workbench would keep it from despawning, and drop it there, where it despawns as usual - all on one pile, beside what the village's Vikings dropped before while any of it is still lying there - and no Viking picks up what was dropped that way again. With nowhere to drop it - an enemy by the pile, say - they hold on to it and try again every 15 seconds, and only after 3 minutes put it down where they stand. Only what they pick up off the ground is dropped - they never take what they don't use out of a chest, so whatever you've stored stays, however much of it there is
- A Viking who carries more than they can sweats and is held back the way you are when you're encumbered - slowed to a walk, and unable to run, jump or block - and lightens their load before anything else: the heaviest thing they can do without first, anything they carry for the village or anything of their own besides what they wear, their shield, their best melee weapon and bow, their arrows, their torch, and their circlet and the helmet it stands in for. They put it away in the nearest chest with room within 20 meters, or with none that near, drop it where they stand for someone else to carry off, only as many as they have to. In a fight, or running from one, shedding the weight comes before fighting - they drop what they can do without on the spot

### Cooking

- They cook. Ahead of all their other work but eating and resting when they're hurt, keeping warm in freezing weather, and loot, a Viking with raw meat - or with raw meat in the village's chests within reach - goes to a lit cooking station with room, taking on the other stations at the same fire no other Viking is at too, so they cook all they can at once - putting it on a piece at a time, waiting by them while it cooks, taking off what's done to put away, burnt coal included, and only once nothing is left cooking on them fetching as much more from the chests as the stations have room for, so nothing burns while they're away. A chest a Viking couldn't get to in the last 10 minutes they leave out, until one opens it, so they don't set off for raw meat they can't reach. When the fire under them goes out - burned down, or put out by the rain - they stop waiting and leave the food on until it's lit again. One Viking cooks at a fire's stations at a time
- Food already on a station comes first - when the Viking cooking it is called away, the next free Viking takes over, done food before food still cooking, so nothing is left to burn. Done food, which burns next, even has a Viking drop the loot they're putting away to take it off first, and a Viking resting or gathering by the fire, talking, repairing, building back, stocking wood or keeping guard through a raid leaves that a moment to take it off, then goes back to it. While you have their inventory open or are naming them, they still take done food off a station within reach where they stand, and when it's out of reach they leave you to take it off, rather than letting it burn
- At dusk they take off what's on the stations before gathering, without putting more on, and food still cooking into the night is taken off once it's done by the next Viking up and about. A Viking who picks up raw meat as loot by day takes it straight to a cooking station with room instead of a chest, and puts the rest of what they picked up away along with the cooked meat - unless they can't get to the station, which they leave alone for a minute, putting the meat in a chest instead
- With nothing left to cook on the stations, a Viking makes a meal at the village's cauldron by day - the one that feeds best, by health and stamina, of the meals the cauldron's level allows, the chests hold everything for, and the chests hold fewer than 10 of. They fetch what it takes from the chests, work at the cauldron the way you craft, and put the meal away in a chest, where the Vikings eat it like any food. Cooking on the stations leaves the chests enough raw food for one of each meal the cauldron can make and the chests hold fewer than 10 of - the raw meat sausages and boar jerky take, say - and a Viking carrying raw meat the chests are short of puts it in a chest rather than on a station. Their Cooking skill rises as they do. One Viking cooks at the cauldron at a time

### Hunting

- They hunt. Now and then the village's archer goes out after prey up to 60 meters past the village's edge, fighting greylings along the way - or, when the village has no archer, any Viking goes, after the prey that doesn't take an archer. Each biome has its own prey, and a Viking hunts that of the biome their gear comes from and every biome before it - so a Black Forest Viking hunts greylings but not the Swamp's leeches and draugr - but only on ground no harder than their own biome, so a Meadows Viking never goes out onto the Black Forest's ground or the Plains', and lets go of prey that runs onto it:

  | Biome | Prey | Only for an archer |
  |---|---|---|
  | Meadows | Boar, necks | Deer |
  | Black Forest | Greylings | |
  | Swamp | Draugr | Leeches |
  | Mountains | Wolves | Drakes |
  | Plains | Lox | Deathsquitos |
  | Mistlands | Hares | |
  | Ashlands | Asksvin | Voltures |
  | Deep North | Moose, seals | |

- Leeches, draugr, wolves, drakes, lox, deathsquitos, Asksvin, voltures and moose fight back hard, so a Viking only goes after one with no other monster within 50 meters of it - not even another of its kind, since angering one of a herd of lox could have the whole herd on them. Only a plain draugr is hunted, never an elite or an archer, and its entrails go into sausages. Leeches keep to themselves, so other leeches nearby don't put a hunter off one. The young of a herd are never hunted. Hunters keep out of the water, shooting a leech from the bank, and leave one too far out to reach
- They stalk their prey: from 60 meters off they crouch and creep up on it like you sneaking - quiet, since crouching makes no sound for it to hear, and harder to see the more they've learned of Sneak and the darker it is, the skill rising as they go - and come at its back when getting there takes swinging no more than 60 degrees around it and the ground lets them, and straight on otherwise, rather than circling all the way around. Livestock in its pen they just walk up to
- An archer shoots their prey from afar: they close in to their bow's range, nearer while something's in the way, then stand with their bow drawn, aim ahead of it and above it for the arrow's drop, and loose an arrow every 2.5 seconds. The range is as far as the arrow flies in 0.7 seconds, and no farther than where the bow's spread keeps it within half a meter of the aim - about 25 meters with a crude bow, 30 with a finewood bow, 35 with a huntsman bow, 42 with a Draugr fang, spinesnap or Deep North bow, and 50 with a crossbow, as far as they see. Once the prey comes within 10 meters, or after 8 arrows, they fight it the usual way. They gather what drops from the kill and from the zombie it rises as, and bring the meat straight to the cooking stations
- An archer also shoots birds - seagulls, crows and the like - when one has landed and is nearer than any prey: they get within 20 meters, draw their bow, aim above the bird for the arrow's drop, and loose an arrow every 2.5 seconds while it's on the ground, giving up after 6, then gather the feathers where it fell. They never shoot at a bird in the air - when it takes off, they follow it with their bow drawn and wait up to 20 seconds for it to land again before giving up on it. Birds take off from the ground when a Viking comes within 4 meters, the same as they do for you, and won't land near one
- They run home from any other enemy that comes near or goes for them, and give up the hunt below half their health. One Viking hunts at a time for every 5 in the village
- While the chests hold fewer than three foods for each Viking - leaving out the food in a chest a Viking couldn't get to in the last 10 minutes, until one opens it - getting food comes ahead of making paths and the village's upkeep, so a village kept busy repairing after attacks still gets meat: a Viking hunts the nearest wild prey they hunt that drops food, leaving alone prey that drops none, like draugr and leeches, and birds - or with no wild prey about that drops food for any of the village's hunters, slaughters one of its livestock. With neither, they go on with their other work, and foraging and hunting come up four times as often when they've nothing to do (see [JOBS.md](JOBS.md#get-food))
- From the Plains on, by the biome their gear comes from, Vikings hunt in a group. Prey that isn't only for archers - lox, wolves, moose and the like - is never hunted alone: the Viking going out brings the nearest 2-3 Vikings free within 30 meters, strolling or standing about and not hurt, or doesn't go. They gather by the leader first, waiting up to 30 seconds, then follow them out as they stalk the prey, and once the leader is within 20 meters of it they all go for it at once. Whichever of them the prey goes after kites it, staying just out of its reach, while the rest fight it - so when it turns on another of them, that one takes over the kiting. The leader gathers what drops from the kill, and the rest go back to their day. Archers still go out alone after what's only for archers, like deathsquitos, and after birds

### Foraging and Bees

- Now and then a Viking with nothing else to do forages - taking the honey from the beehives built in the village first, then picking berries, mushrooms and other food that grows back, what grows back that the cauldron's meals take, like thistle, when the village has a cauldron, and flint, for arrows, up to 60 meters past the village's edge and away from enemies, up to 10 at a time - and puts it away in a chest with room for it, one Viking at a time for every 4 in the village. They pick as much as you would, scaled by the world's resource rate, and get bonus food the way you do from their Farming skill - up to the plant's own chance at full skill, a quarter of the time for most - shown above them with the bonus sound. Each pick raises their Farming like yours. Crops you planted are left alone. When an enemy comes within 50 meters or goes for them while they're out, they run back into the village, where the others help them fight
- Whatever a Viking crafts - arrows, a torch, a hoe - when the village is short of something it takes, they go and get it: foraging for it when it's picked, like flint or stone, or else hunting only the prey or birds that drop it, like greylings for resin, boars for leather or birds for feathers, one Viking at a time for each. With nothing wild about that drops it, they slaughter one of the village's livestock that does, which its pen brings back. An archer with arrows left gets what the best arrows their station allows take before crafting worse ones, and one with none crafts what they can straight away
- They keep bees. Queen bees lying around the village are picked up and put away like other loot, and when the village has fewer than 6 beehives, a Viking with nothing more pressing to do takes a queen bee from a chest, gets the hive's wood - from the chests or the ground, like any building - and builds a beehive on open ground in the village, where it makes honey: under the open sky, off the paths, out of the houses and pens and at least 5 meters from the other hives. One Viking builds a hive at a time. The hives are rebuilt like the rest of the village, and their honey is gathered with the forage

### Free Time

- By day they stroll around the village and stand about, walk up to each other to talk - facing each other, nodding and gesturing as they take turns, trading lines about the dead, the gods and village life, and about the night, the weather, a recent fight or their wounds when there's one, shown under their health bars so only the Vikings near you are seen talking - and pet the village's boars, one Viking at a time for every 6 in the village. Which they do is picked at random, by weight, when there's no work left (see [Rolled Jobs](JOBS.md#rolled-jobs))

### Crafting and Gear

- Every Viking wants both a bow and a melee weapon. One with no bow crafts a crude bow at the workbench once the village has the wood for it and a first batch of arrows. One with only a bow crafts a flint axe while the village has any flint, gathering more when it's short, or else a stone axe while there's stone in the chests or on the ground to gather, or else a club. Better weapons they find replace their own of the same kind
- A Viking with a bow crafts their own arrows. Whenever they're missing at least a batch of their 100 by day, they craft the best arrows the village can make - the ones that deal the most damage among those with a recipe whose station the village has, at the station's level, and whose ingredients other than wood are in the village's chests: flint arrows from flint, fire arrows from resin and feathers, bronze or iron arrows at the forge from bars and feathers, and so on, or else wood arrows. They take the ingredients from the chests, get the wood, work at the station and craft a batch, the best again each time, until their quiver is nearly full, and put back whatever ingredients they didn't use
- Arrows come in three tiers: a Viking always crafts their first 20, cutting brush trees for the wood when the village has none; crafts up to 40 as long as there's wood to get; and only crafts more, up to a full quiver of 100, while the village's chests hold 40 more wood than a batch takes
- They repair their own gear at the village's workbench and forge, the way you do. When a piece one of them can repair - one crafted or repaired there, like a club, a bow or leather armor at the workbench, or bronze and iron at the forge, upgraded far enough - is worn below 75%, they work at it and repair everything the station can, a piece at a time, for free
- They craft gear better than their own - any weapon, armor or shield the game has a recipe for, bucklers included - when the village's chests hold everything it takes, wood included, and the station its recipe needs is upgraded far enough. Better gear is gear they'd take up from a chest over what they have, by the same rules, and they make the best of each kind they can - a melee weapon first, then a bow, a shield, and armor for their chest, legs, head and shoulders - then put it on, carrying what it replaces to a chest. They never go gathering for it, so it's mostly what you give them: put the bronze in a chest and a Viking forges a bronze sword. One Viking of the village arms themselves at a time, by day, after their other work
- They upgrade their own gear the way you do, when the village has everything it takes. A piece below its top quality is raised one quality at the station its recipe is crafted at - the workbench or forge - once that station is upgraded far enough for it, taking what the game's recipe asks for that quality from the village's chests, wood included, and leaving the piece at full durability. Their lowest quality gear goes first, and what they have equipped before what they carry, one Viking of the village at a time, by day, after their other work
- The village keeps tools in storage: 3 axes, counting the ones its Vikings carry, and from the Black Forest on, 4 pickaxes and an axe good enough for fine wood. Once nothing more pressing is left to do, a Viking crafts the best one the village has everything but wood for and puts it away - or for an axe, a stone axe when it can make nothing better, getting the stone first when the village is short. They never craft a battleaxe for the chests

### The Village's Goal

- Each village works toward one piece of gear at a time - the next step up for the Viking with the weakest gear, an upgrade of what they wear or a better piece they'd take up, that the village can get - and works back from what it takes to the next thing to do. Ore in the chests is smelted, coal burnt in the kiln from wood when the smelter needs it, and what the gear is made from, like bronze, crafted at the forge. A Viking takes the ore and fuel from the chests, puts the fuel in the smelter, then the ore, a piece at a time, the way you do, and the Vikings pick up the bars it drops and put them away. One Viking smelts at a time, they top up a smelter that runs out of fuel before its ore is done, and they leave 20 wood in the chests rather than burn it all for coal
- What they can't get at home, they go out on a quest for (see [Quests](#quests))

## Quests

What they can't get at home, they go out on a quest for, out of your sight - when the next thing their goal needs is something like ore to mine or a crypt to raid, and their biome isn't easier than the quest's: a Meadows village never goes mining. They also go hunting or foraging, taking turns, when their chests are low on food - fewer than three foods for each Viking - and they've found no food around the village for half a day - picked none, and gone for no prey that drops any - or the chests hold less than one food for each Viking, when food comes before their goal, so the wounded have something to heal on; cutting trees when they're out of wood and find none to gather; to the nearest trader they can sell to - Hildir from the Meadows on, Haldor from the Black Forest on and the Bog Witch from the Swamp on - to sell their amber, pearls, rubies and silver necklaces once they're worth 100 coins; and to Haldor, each at most once a week, when the village's coins and treasure will pay for it - from the Black Forest on to buy a Dverger circlet for a Viking still using torches and a strength belt for one without, from the Mountains on wider pockets for one without, and from the Mistlands on deeper pockets for one with wider pockets only - the one who needs it going, and wearing it home or having the pockets sewn in there. A village that can't be left goes hunting, foraging or cutting trees as soon as it runs low. Every detail of each quest is in [QUESTS.md](QUESTS.md).

- A quest takes as many Vikings as it needs - more for one that takes a pickaxe or axe while the village has more of them - as long as 2 stay home, and the healthiest go, those with the tool first. Picked late in the day, the Vikings wait for the next morning and set out as they wake

While they're gone the village counts them as its own, so no one comes in their place

Coming home to find everyone else in the village dead, they cry out and mourn by name those who died while they were gone

## Vikings With No Village

A living Viking who belongs to no village and isn't near one - spawned out in the wild, or leaving a village you disbanded - makes for the village they can get to soonest, over land and sea and through portals.

### Finding a Village

- One you spawn out in the wild with a console command - `spawn Viking`, or another mod's spawn command - is lost at first: they stand still for 3 seconds, then turn to look a new way every 3-4 seconds, for 15-20 seconds in all. A second or two after they appear they say something like "Where am I?" or "Am I in Valhalla?", and something else 7 seconds later. Once they've worked out where to go, they say so as they set out - "Eikvik! That's where I'll go.", naming the village, or that they think there's a village that way when it has no name - or that there's nowhere near and they'll have to wander. Several spawned together decide as a group - and so do any Vikings with no village within 20 meters of each other who have to plan their road again, like after a wreck: the first of them to come to gathers the others still lost within 20 meters, works out where they're all going - keeping out of biomes harder than the easiest of them and sailing in the best boat of the hardest - and says so, and they set out together as one group, waiting for each other on the road and crossing the sea in the boats one of them builds. They can still greet you, the way a traveler does - lost, they ask you where they are. One spawned another way skips straight to finding a village
- They pick the village of their own biome or easier that they can get to soonest, anywhere in the world: one of the Meadows' farms, whether it has been generated yet or not - but for one that stayed abandoned with no room for a bonfire - or a village a player started
- They go on foot, swimming rivers and swamp water as far as their stamina takes them, and cross the sea by boat - and any water deep enough for a boat that's too wide to swim, like the deep water between an island and the mainland, which the world counts as the shore rather than the sea. Getting into a boat takes a while, so water they can swim across they swim. They take the best boat their biome has: a raft from the Meadows, a karve from the Black Forest and the Swamp, a longship from the Mountains to the Mistlands, and a drakkar from the Ashlands on - but never the Ashlands' lava or the edge of the world, and never the Ashlands' boiling sea in a boat it would burn, which only the drakkar holds up in. They also go through any portal joined to another, yours or a village's, when that gets them there sooner - but not while one of them carries something a portal won't take, like ore, unless the portal takes everything or the world lets every portal, and never in a world with portals turned off. So the village they pick is the soonest there by land, sea and portal, not the nearest in a straight line
- The server plans the road over the world's map, ground no one has been near included, keeping out of biomes harder than theirs where there's a way around. A group that sets out together keeps out of biomes harder than the easiest of them and sails in the best boat of the hardest of them
- With no village they can reach, they wander - strolling a short way at a time over ground no harder than their biome, away from starred monsters and those much tougher than them, fighting what they can take on, picking food and picking up loot lying within 10 meters outside any player's base - and look for a village again every 3 minutes

### On the Road

- On the road they run from waypoint to waypoint, with a torch out at night, and stay out of every fight they can - running from an enemy that comes within 30 meters, along their road when that leads away from it - unless they can't get away: the enemy is faster than them, they're cornered, or it's hurting them up close. They stand and fight then, and join in when a player or another living Viking within 15 meters is attacked. A group that set out together waits for anyone who falls 15 meters behind. They eat the food they carry the way they eat from the chests at home - below 75% health the food that heals fastest, and otherwise keeping their foods up on the weakest that does them good (see [Eating](#eating)) - and pick food growing within 25 meters when they have none they'd eat
- One with a cart pulls it along, hooked up the way you pull one - set down behind them again when it snags or comes loose (see [Look After the Cart](JOBS.md#look-after-the-cart) in JOBS.md). They let go of it before they fight or run, and go back for it after. When they're taken off the road, their cart goes with them, holding what it held, and it's back behind them as soon as they're back in the world. A road planned for a group with a cart keeps off rough ground where it can - the Swamp, the Mountains and the Mistlands, each meter of which counts as taking three times as long - so they go around when it isn't far. At a portal they pull their cart up to it, let go and take it down with one swing of a hammer, keeping what it held, step through, and build it back with a swing on the other side, holding the same - or, when they're taken off the road through it, it goes with them. When they carry what the portal won't take - in their packs or their cart, picked up since their road was planned - they plan their road again without that portal. At the shore they take their cart down the same way before they set sail, and build it back where they come ashore. A cart they lose track of, more than 80 meters behind them, is left. When their cart is destroyed, they pick up what spilled from it within 5 meters of where it broke, most useful first, as much as they can carry without being slowed, and build it back from nothing with a swing, putting back in it what they picked up. Reaching the village they're going to, they pull their cart in and it becomes one of the village's, which its Vikings empty into the chests - its own cart when it has none, or else one they take down once it's emptied
- Now and then, with you near, they say things like "I need to get to Eikvik." - a farm not set up yet is named by the name settled for it - and they pass on your news when they greet you, but say none of a village's lines

### Sailing

- Where their road reaches the sea, they wait at the shore for the rest of their group, for up to 30 seconds, then the one of the hardest biome among them walks down to the water's edge, takes out a hammer and builds the boats they need from nothing - the best boat of their biome, or for a Viking crossing alone a karve, which is plenty for one, unless the sea ahead boils and only their drakkar holds up in it - as many as it takes for all of them (a raft carries 6, a karve 10, a longship 25 and a drakkar 44), with one of them at each helm, one holding on to the mast - and on the longship another holding on at the figurehead - and the rest on the ship's stools, then sitting on the boards wherever there's room - on the longship's cargo, and spread evenly along the drakkar's steps - a little crowded on the smaller boats. The boats drop nothing when they break
- Each Viking tries once to jump on board from the shore and swims to the ladder if they miss, and those not on board after 30 seconds are put there. Once they're all on, the boat waits for you if you're within 40 meters - 5 seconds later the Viking at the helm asks if you're coming, and again every 15 seconds, and 15 seconds after the first time it sets off with or without you. If you're swimming out to it, it waits for up to a minute and a half. On board another boat, you've gone your own way, so it doesn't wait for you - once every player near is on a boat, it sets off. When it sets off without you, the Viking at the helm calls out a farewell. Once you climb aboard, it sets off 2 seconds later
- You can ride along, but the helm is theirs. Walk into a Viking sitting on the boards and they scoot out of your way, still sitting, up to a little over a meter - never off the deck or into the mast, the rail or anything else on board - and slide slowly back to their seat once you've moved on. Those at the helm, holding on to the mast or the figurehead, or on a stool or the drakkar's steps stay put. A seat a Viking has - holding the mast, on a stool, or a chair at home - is in use, so the game won't let you take it too, and one who comes to their seat with you already in it, holding the mast, say, sits on the boards instead
- The boat sails itself along their road, backing off when it gets stuck. It sails by the world's wind the way yours does: faster in a strong wind than a light one, and fastest with the wind from the side. Heading into the wind it makes its way at half the speed a wind from behind would give it, never stopping dead the way yours does. While anyone on board is under Moder's power - one of the crew, who use it when the boat heads into the wind (see [Forsaken Powers](#forsaken-powers)), or you - the wind blows from behind it. A Viking knocked out of their seat sits back down while they're still on board
- They talk as they sail - one of them on board every 35-70 seconds, about what's around them: flying monsters after them first, then a sea serpent after them - one within 60 meters going for someone on board, or within 30 - then a leviathan in sight within 120 meters, then the weather - a storm, rain, the cold or fog - then the night, and otherwise the calm sea and fair wind. Something new to worry about, or a leviathan, is called out as soon as they see it, never within 8 seconds of the last line. As the boat reaches the far shore, one of them calls out land, naming the village they're going to
- They fight off what flies at them and what rises from the sea, from the deck. When a flying monster - a deathsquito, a drake, a bat - or a sea serpent attacks the boat, going after anyone on board or one of them overboard, or coming within 15 meters - 30 for a serpent, which circles a boat before it strikes - every Viking with a bow but the one at the helm, who keeps steering, stands up at their seat and shoots at the nearest one within 40 meters, aiming ahead of it and over it for the drop, an arrow every 2.5 seconds. They never leave the boat for it - knocked off their feet more than 2 meters from their seat, they're back in it at once - and sit back down 3 seconds after the last of them is gone. Those without a bow stay in their seats
- They don't leave anyone in the water. When one of them or a player who was riding along goes overboard, the Viking at the helm calls out their name and the boat comes about for them - turning toward them at half sail, slowing within 30 meters and stopping within 8 - and sails on once they're back on board, ashore or dead, or after 3 minutes it can't reach them. A Viking who goes overboard cries for help as they hit the water, swims for the boat's nearest ladder and climbs back to their seat, saying they're glad to be back aboard, when their stamina will get them there, and otherwise treads water, which takes no stamina, calling out now and then until the boat comes near enough
- In the water, they climb onto whatever boat is nearest - their own, another group's, or yours. When another boat's ladder is nearer than their own's, or theirs is gone and a boat within 60 meters is nearer than land, they swim for it, climb up and sit on its deck out of the way, leaving their own boat to sail on without them. They ride along wherever it goes, and once it has stopped within 30 meters of land for 3 seconds, they jump off, swim ashore and carry on from there - planning their road again, or going on with their life for a village's Viking
- At the far shore the boat comes in at slow speed as close as it can - to within 6 meters of where they go ashore, stopping short where the water ahead gets shallower than 1.5 meters, or once it's aground and gets no nearer for 4 seconds - and they all jump off and swim in, and the one who was at the helm breaks the boat up with their hammer - waiting for you to get off first, for up to a minute. When the village they're going to stands on that shore - within 80 meters of where they come ashore - and has no boat of that kind by it yet, the village's own moored one, yours or one left before, they leave the boat there for the village instead, the first of a group's boats, the rest broken up as before. Once they're all off it, it's an ordinary ship, like the village's own moored one: yours to take the helm of, and dropping what it's built from when it breaks. If the boat is wrecked at sea, they swim for the nearest shore, or a boat nearer than that, and once ashore they plan their road again, building another boat if there's still sea to cross. They never build a boat out in the water, where it's 1.5 meters deep or more - one whose road takes to the sea where they stand gets out onto the nearest boat or ashore first. Log out on their boat in single player and it's all still there when you come back

### Off the Road

- Where there's no water deep enough for a boat by the shore, they say they're setting sail and are taken off the road to sail the rest of the way. Where it reaches a portal, they walk up to it, say they're stepping through, and once all of them are there step through together - coming out the other side right away when a player is near it, or else taken off the road the same way
- A group is never split up: as soon as one of them goes out of every player's reach, the whole group is taken off the road. The server follows them along their road while they're off it, so when a portal on the way is broken or no longer joined to its other end, they find another way from wherever they've got to
- The server keeps them until they'd have covered the rest of the way - running at about 5 meters a second, sailing at their boat's speed, from about 3 meters a second for a raft to 8.5 for a drakkar, and 5 seconds for each portal - a group as one, so they arrive together. A group with a cart takes longer over land by what its heaviest cart holds - a quarter longer with a full load of 800 weight, and never more than half again as long (see [Taking the Cart](QUESTS.md#taking-the-cart) in QUESTS.md). One who dies on the road is gone for good

### Arriving

- Once they're due and their village is loaded and set up, they walk in from the side they came from, out of sight, as they were - with their gear, inventory, skills and the players they know, and a few things they picked on the way: one for every 3 minutes they walked through a biome where something grows - raspberries and mushrooms in the Meadows, blueberries, mushrooms, yellow mushrooms and thistle in the Black Forest, cloudberries in the Plains, Jotun puffs and magecaps in the Mistlands, fiddleheads and smoke puffs in the Ashlands - up to 5 each. Hunters also take a prey for every 5 minutes they walk through a biome with prey, up to 2 each - a lox three eighths as often and a moose or seal half as often - and bring home what it drops. They only find things in biomes no harder than their own, so a Meadows Viking crossing the Plains finds nothing there. Vikings out to mine or raid only pick on the way home. Out of reach they eat on the road the way they would in sight - going on with the foods they'd eaten, eating what they carry when they're not hurt the way they keep their foods up at home, and what they find once they've found it - and their food heals them every 10 seconds the whole way, so they walk in still having eaten what hasn't worn off and as healed as their food made them. A village no one goes near keeps them waiting, and their food doesn't wear off while they wait
- A Viking who reaches their village joins it once it has had every Viking it starts with, waiting by it while a farm no one has been near is set up. One who reaches a farm that stayed abandoned, or a village that's gone, says so and moves on to the next nearest - or, for one who left a village you disbanded, to a village picked at random - and one who waits 5 minutes by a village that should take them but has no house for them moves on to the next nearest
- For 12 game hours after they get where they were going - a village they join, or home from a quest - they talk about the trip, to you and to the others, along with how their quest went: the thing most worth telling, from being wrecked at sea, turning around for home, finding their village gone or a broken portal on their road, to a portal they stepped through, the boat they sailed in, the hardest biome they walked through or what they picked on the way

## News

Word of what you do gets around, and the living Vikings pass it on - each piece of news told by 2-4 Vikings, from any village, each in a line of their own, in place of their greeting or what they'd say when you talk to them (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)).

- News is made when you kill a boss or a sea serpent (the Bonemaw too), drop a tree on a monster - a boss included - sail within 30 meters of a leviathan, die, or sail off the edge of the world, which the Vikings only admire you for and don't count as a death
- And by your character's firsts in the world: the first time they kill each of Hildir's minibosses (Brenna, Geirrhafa, and Zil and Thungr once both are down), talk to or trade with Haldor, Hildir and the Bog Witch, go into a burial chamber, troll cave or frost cave, discover each biome but the Meadows - when the game says you've discovered it, counting a biome's alternate versions as the biome itself - tame each kind of animal, take each skill to 100, have every skill at 100, and earn each of these achievements: The Lumberjack, The Cook, The Farmer, The Forager, The Beekeeper, No Adventuring On an Empty Stomach, The Hunter, The Trophy Collector, The Fisherman, A Catch Worthy of Njord, The Blacksmith, The Artisan, The Master Chef, The Gourmet, The Chosen One, Mighty Halls, Top of the World, The Carpenter, The Explorer, The Sailor, The Captain, Kings and Queens of Valheim, Child of Odin and That Can't Be an Accident. The game only says you've discovered a biome, and awards an achievement, once for each character, whatever the world - so a character who found the Swamp in another world isn't news for it here
- A kill is news to every player who hit the creature, and a tree's to the player who felled it - or who last hit its log, which sent it rolling. A tame is news to the player nearest the animal, and the rest to the player they happened to. Nothing done by cheating or with console commands is news - a creature the game marks as killed by someone cheating, the console's kill and tame commands, or a skill raised with `raiseskill`
- A Viking tells you your news when they greet you or you talk to them - 90% of the time, only the latest they haven't told you, and only news from after they last talked to you, so a Viking you've just met has none for you, and talking to a Viking again right after has them chat as usual. A Viking who doesn't know you yet only greets you. The Vikings who tell an event each say a different line about it, and once 2-4 of them have, it's no longer news
- A boss killed again takes the place of its news, told to everyone who has killed it while it was news, and a death, a leviathan, a sea serpent or a monster killed with a tree takes the place of your last news of that kind. News is forgotten after 10 game days, told or not
- News of a boss or biome beyond a Viking's own village's biome - the Plains' Yagluth to a Mountain village, say - has them add how impressed they are
- The server keeps the news, saved with the world, and every player's game gets it from the server, so the Vikings of every village know it
- The villages hear from each other too: when a village is disbanded or started, a quest comes home with something worth talking about, a Viking dies or a newcomer arrives, the villages within 3 km hear about it for 3 game days - by rune stick or raven, or from a Viking who came from there, who brought the news themselves. Now and then a Viking tells you, or talks it over with another - a village being disbanded most of all, and a Viking who came from a village most of all about that one

## Dvergr Vikings

Some Vikings have taken work with the Dvergr in the Mistlands. A Dvergr Viking is a living Viking in the Dvergr's pay, who keeps to their outposts and patrols and never joins a village.

- Each of the Dvergr's outposts hires some, once: the first time you've been within it for 5 seconds with its Dvergr up, it hires anywhere from none to a quarter of the Dvergr still alive there, rounded to the nearest, each number as likely, up to 4 - none with 1 Dvergr, up to 1 with 2-5, up to 2 with 6-9, and so on. They appear beside its Dvergr, keeping to their post when the Dvergr beside them do. An outpost never hires again, whether they live or die, and one whose Dvergr were killed before the mod came along hires none
- Half of the Dvergr patrols the world spawns have one along, who wanders with them
- They're on the Dvergr's side - the Dvergr, Dvergr Vikings and living Vikings never hurt each other. Like the Dvergr, they can be hit with PvP off, and the console's kill commands kill them. Hurt one, or a Dvergr near one, and every Dvergr, Dvergr Viking and living Viking within 20 meters turns on you - and like the Dvergr, a Dvergr Viking stays angry
- They fight like zombie Vikings: the health and damage of a Mistlands zombie Viking, one star as often as the game gives the creatures it spawns there, and a pause between swings - but their armor protects them in full, and a monster that catches them unawares gets no sneak attack bonus on them, and the damage they take is scaled the way a living Viking's is. Unlike a living Viking, a staggered Dvergr Viking takes a critical hit, double damage, like any other creature. They don't take fall damage, like the Dvergr they live with
- Like living Vikings, they have the Mistlands' gear, fully repaired, with a Dverger circlet they light at night and a wisp light against the mist, and a name of their own - they're plain Viking to you until they know you. Their skills rise as they use them, and they see no farther in the mist than living Vikings, but for the wisp light they carry
- They have words of their own, about working for the Dvergr and how they came to be hired - greeting you as you arrive and when you talk to them, calling out the enemies they go after, shouting for help when they're badly hurt, and passing on your news the way living Vikings do, sharing only the living Vikings' lines about the Mistlands, about old friends and how you're doing, and about the runestones where they stand and of someone with their name (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)). They stand facing you for 4 seconds when you talk to them, and remember when they met you and how many times you've talked, the way living Vikings do
- You can talk to them, but can't name them or open their inventory
- They know the forsaken powers up to the Queen's, as Mistlands Vikings, learn the ones they're given, and use them in fights and when badly hurt (see [Forsaken Powers](#forsaken-powers))
- When they die they drop 100-140 coins and 0-2 entrails, multiplied by their stars, and each piece of their gear has a chance to drop (see [Configuration](README.md#configuration))

## With Zombie Apocalypse

With [Zombie Apocalypse](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Kriona/ZombieApocalypse/) installed too, the living Vikings live through its apocalypse:

- A living or Dvergr Viking who dies rises as a zombie Viking with their looks, name, gear and skills, as any slain creature does under Zombie Apocalypse's rules - their name showing after the zombie word to the players they knew, like Zombie Ragnar, and plain Zombie Viking to anyone else - and drops coins like any zombie Viking when killed in turn - along with the wood and loot they carried for their village
- They stand ready over the corpses about to rise, fight the zombie once it's up rather than a corpse that can't be hurt, and keep clear of the poison clouds zombies' corpses leave (see [The Dead](#the-dead))
- They talk of the dead walking (see [DIALOG.md](DIALOG.md)) - of burning the dead only when Zombie Apocalypse's `FireStaysDead` lets fire keep them down, and of silver whenever they do, since spirit damage always keeps the dead down
- The empty abandoned houses near a farm are its Vikings', so zombie Vikings never hold them
- A name with Zombie Apocalypse's zombie word in it is one they turn down
- From the Mountains on, a living or Dvergr Viking may start with silver-lined weapons - each weapon that can be lined at the forge is lined 25% of the time in the Mountains, 50% in the Plains and 75% in the Mistlands, and always from the Ashlands on. An archer whose arrows deal no spirit damage starts with 50 silver arrows by the same chance, when their bow would hurt the undead more with them
